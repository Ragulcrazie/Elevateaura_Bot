Why families cannot do this alone

A catheter or feeding tube is not something to learn from a video.

These are the tasks that scare families most, and rightly so. A mistake with a catheter or a Ryles tube can cause infection, injury or a dangerous feed.

Insertion is risky

Placing a catheter or NG tube wrong can injure or introduce infection.

Feeding fears

Feeding through a misplaced tube is genuinely dangerous.

Blockage and leaks

Blocked catheters and tube problems arrive without warning.

Infection worry

Poor hygiene around a catheter quickly leads to a UTI.

Common questions

Catheter and Ryles tube care, answered.

Can a nurse insert a urinary catheter at home?

Yes. A registered nurse can insert, change and care for a urinary (Foley) catheter at home using sterile technique, on the doctor's advice.

Do you handle Ryles tube feeding?

Yes. Ryles (nasogastric) tube insertion, safe feeding, flushing and hygiene are provided at home, with the family taught how to feed between visits if needed.

How often should a catheter be changed?

Typically every few weeks depending on the type and the doctor's plan, or sooner if there is blockage or infection. The nurse tracks this and changes it on time.

How do you prevent catheter infections?

Sterile insertion, regular hygiene, correct drainage-bag handling and watching for signs of infection at each visit reduce the risk considerably.

Is this part of bedridden and elderly care?

Yes. It is often combined with elderly home care and 24-hour nursing for dependent patients.
