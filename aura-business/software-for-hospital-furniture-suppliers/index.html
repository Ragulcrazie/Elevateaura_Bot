Why a furniture order behaves like a project

Hospital furniture is rarely bought one bed at a time. A new hospital block, a ward refurbishment or a government tender produces a single order for hundreds of identical items, and that order is then released slowly, because the wards are painted and handed over in sequence. Software for hospital furniture suppliers has to hold one order against many part deliveries, so everybody can see what has gone, what is due and what has been billed.

The service story is also different from equipment. Beds, trolleys and cabinets are mechanical rather than electronic, so the obligation is usually a warranty with a stock of replacement castors, actuators, side rails and hydraulic parts, not a maintenance contract with scheduled visits. Aura Business tracks the order in phases, sends installation crews with job cards, and records warranty claims against the item and the ward it was installed in.

Where project orders go wrong

One order, a dozen deliveries, and no single view of it.

The order value makes the business. The margin is lost in transport done twice, items delivered to the wrong floor and installation labour standing idle.

Balance quantities disputed

After six part deliveries nobody agrees on how many beds are still due against the original order.

Deliveries ahead of the site

A consignment reaches a hospital where the ward is not plastered, and the stock sits in a corridor at your risk.

Challans and invoices out of step

Goods moved on challans over three months, and invoicing is reconstructed later from a pile of paper.

Installation crews unmanaged

Assembly teams are sent without a list of what is on site, so half a day is spent finding cartons.

Warranty claims untraceable

A bed fails in its second year and nobody can say when it was supplied or whether it is still in warranty.

Payment tied to milestones

Payment follows delivery and installation stages, and the paperwork proving each stage is scattered.

Workflow: Every challan is raised against the sales order line, so the order shows ordered, delivered and pending quantities at any moment.

Installation is proved

Questions

Hospital furniture supply, answered.

Can one order be delivered over several months?

Yes. The sales order holds the full quantity and each release goes out on its own delivery challan, so ordered, delivered and pending quantities are visible on the order at any time without adding up paperwork.

Do we need AMC contracts for furniture?

Usually not. Most furniture obligations are warranty rather than scheduled maintenance, so the platform records the warranty period per supplied item and raises a job when a claim comes in. Annual contracts are available if you sell them on powered beds.

How is installation labour tracked across a large site?

Installation is raised as jobs per ward or floor, assigned to a crew with a date, and closed on the mobile job card with photographs and the ward in-charge signature. Offline mode covers sites without network.

Can we hold stock at the hospital site?

Yes. Create the site store as a warehouse location and transfer stock to it. Movements are recorded, so what is on site and what has been installed are separate and countable numbers.

Does it handle government tender supply?

Yes. The tender tracker holds documents, EMD and deadlines, rate contracts govern the price on repeat supply, and outcome analytics show your win rate by category. See tender management software.

How are replacement parts for beds managed?

Castors, side rails, actuators and similar parts are stock items with reorder levels, and a part used on a warranty job is issued against that job so stock drops and the claim history builds against the item.
