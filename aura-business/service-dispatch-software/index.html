In one sentence

What the dispatcher's screen has to show

Service dispatch software is the allocation screen. Every open service call sits on it, ordered by priority and by how much SLA time is left, with the customer, the asset, the fault reported and whether the device is under warranty or contract already attached. The dispatcher picks an engineer and the job lands on that engineer's mobile app.

In Aura Business this is the Technician Dispatch module in the Field Service suite, sitting between call intake and the mobile job card. It matters because the dispatcher is making the same decision forty times a morning, and the cost of getting it wrong is a wasted journey or a breached contract.

Why allocation goes wrong

Dispatch done on a phone call is dispatch done blind.

The dispatcher who works from memory knows the engineers well and the calls badly, or the other way round. Neither is enough by nine in the morning.

No single list of open calls

Some calls came by phone, some by WhatsApp, some through a rep, and none of them are in the same place.

Priority set by who shouted

The loudest customer gets the engineer while a contract nearing SLA breach waits.

The wrong engineer sent

Someone who has never worked on that model drives across the city and comes back without fixing it.

Three trips to the same area

Two engineers cross each other because nobody looked at the calls by location.

Reassignment loses the thread

A call is handed to another engineer over the phone and the original notes never arrive with it.

No idea where anyone is

At three in the afternoon the dispatcher is calling engineers to ask whether they are free.

In practice

Four dispatch decisions that get easier.

The morning allocation takes twenty minutes

Workflow: Open calls are already ranked by SLA time left, and each one shows the equipment, the location and whether it is under contract.

Result: The board is cleared before the engineers leave home.

The right engineer goes the first time

Workflow: A ventilator call is matched to an engineer trained on that line rather than whoever is nearest.

Result: Fewer second visits for the same fault.

Two calls in one area are combined

Workflow: The route view shows both hospitals on the same side of the city and puts them on one engineer's day.

Result: Less travel, more calls closed.

A sick day does not break the day

Workflow: One engineer's calls are reassigned in bulk, each carrying its notes, history and remaining SLA time.

Result: Customers do not repeat themselves to a new engineer.

Questions

Service dispatch, answered.

Does the dispatcher see where engineers are?

Yes. GPS and field tracking shows engineer location during working hours, and check-in happens against the customer site, so allocation can take the current position into account.

Can allocation be based on skill rather than distance?

Yes. Skill-based technician allocation lets you record which engineers work on which equipment lines, so a specialised call is not given to the nearest available person by default.

What does the engineer receive on the phone?

The job appears in the mobile app with the customer, site, asset and serial number, the fault reported, the cover status and the previous service history for that machine. See mobile job cards.

Can a call be moved to another engineer mid-day?

Yes. Reassignment keeps the call's notes, attachments, SLA clock and history, so the new engineer starts with everything the first one had.

How does the SLA affect the queue?

Each contract carries its own response and resolution times. The clock on a call orders the board and flags calls approaching breach. See SLA management.

Does the app work where there is no signal?

Yes. The engineer can complete the job card offline inside a basement or a shielded room and it syncs when the phone reconnects.
