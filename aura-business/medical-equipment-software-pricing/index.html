In one sentence

How Aura Business is priced, and why the figure follows a call

Aura Business is quoted after a short call, because the number follows the scope you switch on rather than a published list. The model itself is plain: you pay for how many of the 87 modules you turn on, in three tiers of 15, 47 or all 87, and every tier carries unlimited users. Office staff, technicians and management are never counted, so the bill does not move when the field team grows.

The rest of a quote is the work around the software: migrating the spreadsheets you run on today, putting your branding on the dashboard, the mobile app and your invoices, and any customisation or integration your trade needs. Those are defined pieces of work rather than a running meter.

This is why two businesses of similar size are quoted differently. One arrives with three tidy workbooks and wants the standard AMC workflow. The other has years of service history in mixed formats and a quotation document its hospitals already expect. Same platform, different scope, different quote.

Why per-seat pricing punishes a growing field team

A seat licence is a tax on hiring. The technicians who most need the mobile job card are the ones you add last, so the people at the edge of the business end up sharing a login or staying on paper, and the service history you bought the system for never gets recorded. Aura Business is never billed per seat for exactly that reason. Every technician, storekeeper and accounts clerk can hold their own role-based login on day one.

Why the tier matters more than anything else

Most of the difference between one quote and another is simply how much platform is switched on. A dealer who needs quotations, GST invoices and serial-level stock is buying a different system from a national distributor running tenders, procurement, payroll inputs and a distributor portal. Start with the modules that carry your revenue today, and use the module store to switch more on when the business asks for them rather than when a sales cycle does.

Before you sign anything

Compare on total cost of ownership, not the headline figure

The quote you accept is rarely what you end up paying. Put every vendor through the same list and the differences show up quickly.

  • Is the licence per user? Ask what the bill looks like after you hire five technicians, and again after twenty.
  • Which modules are in this quote? Get the list in writing, then ask what is charged when you need one that is missing.
  • Who moves the old data? Ask whether migration of your spreadsheets sits inside the quote or arrives later as a project.
  • Is training chargeable per branch? A system nobody at the counter can drive is an expense, not an asset.
  • What does support actually cover? Ask which channel, who answers, and what falls outside it.
  • What do you take with you if you leave? Settle the export format for your customers, assets and service history before you sign, not after.

Against spreadsheets, the real cost is invisible

A workbook costs nothing and loses money quietly. The bill arrives as AMC contracts that lapsed because nobody was reminded, warranty claims that expired unnoticed, spares issued against no job, and invoices retyped from a quotation with a fresh error each time. Before you compare software quotes, put a rough figure on a year of that leakage in your own business. Our honest look at where the line falls is in spreadsheets versus distributor software.

Against per-user tools, compare the same five years

A generic CRM sold per user looks light next to an operations platform until you add the field team, the second warehouse and the add-ons for service and billing. Line up both options over the same period, with the headcount you expect to have at the end of it, not the headcount you have today. Then check what the generic tool still cannot do: AMC renewals, serial-level stock, a mobile job card that drops spares, and a GST invoice your auditor accepts.

Questions

Pricing questions we are asked most.

Why don't you publish your prices?

Because a published number would be wrong for almost everyone who read it. Scope varies enormously between a single dealer on the 15-module tier and a distributor running all 87 with years of data to migrate. A figure that misleads you wastes your time and ours, so we scope first and quote once.

How is Aura Business priced?

By how much of the platform you switch on. There are three module tiers, 15, 47 or all 87 modules, and every tier includes unlimited users. Setup work, which covers migrating your existing Excel data and applying your branding, is scoped alongside the tier.

Do you charge per user or per seat?

No. User count does not appear in the quote at all. You can put every technician, storekeeper and accounts person on the system with their own role-based login, and your cost is unchanged when you hire more of them next quarter.

Is data migration included?

Setup includes migrating your existing Excel data. How much work that is depends on how many years and how many formats you are bringing across, so we look at your actual files during scoping rather than assuming. Messy data is normal and is not a problem.

Can we start on a lower tier and add modules later?

Yes. The module store lets you switch further modules on as the business needs them, and moving up a tier is scoped as its own small change. Most businesses begin with the suites that carry revenue today, usually sales, billing, stock and service.

How quickly do we get an actual number?

Usually from one call. A 30-minute demo call is enough to agree the module tier, look at the spreadsheets you run on, and identify any customisation or integration. The quote follows that conversation, with the setup work itemised rather than bundled.
