Why HVAC contracts break a normal service system

An HVAC maintenance contract is rarely about one machine. It covers a fleet: forty split units across an office, a chiller plant and its handling units in a hospital, or every unit in eighteen branches of the same customer. The contract is one agreement, the schedule is per unit, and the invoicing is usually per site or per group. Software for HVAC AMC work has to hold that shape, because a service system that assumes one asset per contract will not survive the first renewal.

The second difficulty is that the work is seasonal. Everybody wants their planned maintenance finished before the weather turns, which compresses a year of visits into a few weeks and then fills the following months with breakdown calls. Aura Business schedules planned visits per unit, plans engineer routes across sites, tracks filter and consumable replacement separately from the visit itself, and raises renewals before the season rather than after it.

The seasonal squeeze

A year of planned work compressed into six weeks.

The problems of an HVAC service business are problems of capacity, repetition and scale rather than of complexity.

The unit list is out of date

The contract covers units installed years ago, some replaced or moved, and the list has never been corrected.

Planned visits run late into the season

The pre-season programme is still unfinished when the breakdown calls start, so it is quietly abandoned.

Engineers criss-cross the city

Visits are assigned by whoever calls first rather than grouped by area, so half the day is travel.

Consumables not planned

Filters and belts are replaced when an engineer notices, so the van does not carry what the site needed.

Multi-site billing is manual

One customer with eighteen branches wants one invoice, or eighteen, and that is assembled by hand each time.

Renewals slip past the season

Contracts lapse in the busiest month and are only noticed when a lapsed customer calls with a breakdown.

Questions

HVAC maintenance contracts, answered.

Can one contract cover hundreds of units across sites?

Yes. A contract covers a listed fleet of assets, and each unit keeps its own location, schedule and service history. Multi-site contracts hold the sites of one customer under a single agreement with reporting across all of them.

How do we get the pre-season programme finished?

Visits are raised per unit against the contract and shown as a programme with due, completed and outstanding counts. Routing groups them by area, so capacity is spent on units rather than on travel.

Can filter and consumable replacement be tracked separately from the visit?

Yes. Consumables can run on their own replacement cycle per unit, and the items are issued against the job when they are fitted, so stock drops and the unit's history shows what was changed and when.

Can a customer's facilities team log calls themselves?

Yes. The customer portal and helpdesk let a facilities team raise a call, see its status and see the visit history for their sites, which also gives you a clean intake time for measuring response.

Does it handle chargeable work outside the contract?

Yes. A call can be recorded as covered or chargeable, with parts and labour billed on a service invoice where they fall outside the agreement. What is covered and excluded is held on the contract for the desk to check at intake.

Do renewals have to be chased manually?

No. The renewal engine works from contract end dates and raises the offer in advance, which matters in a seasonal trade where a lapse during the busy months is rarely noticed until a breakdown call arrives.
