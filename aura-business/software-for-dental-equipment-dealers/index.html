Why dental dealing is a volume problem, not a deal problem

A dental dealer does not manage twenty large accounts. It manages hundreds of small ones, most of them a single practice where the dentist is the buyer, the user and the person who pays. There is no purchase committee and no tender, so the sale turns on how fast a quote arrives and how quickly a chair is fixed. Software for dental equipment dealers has to make hundreds of light relationships manageable rather than make one heavy relationship elaborate.

The money comes in two streams. Chairs, autoclaves, compressors, X-ray units and handpieces are bought occasionally and serviced often. Composites, burs, gloves and impression materials are bought again and again, in small orders, at thin margins. Aura Business gives the clinic a portal to repeat its own consumable order, gives the technician a route and a mobile job card, and gives the office one view of a great many small balances.

What this trade struggles with

Small orders, small balances, and a chair that stopped this morning.

Nothing here is individually difficult. The difficulty is the number of them arriving at once.

The day is spent on the phone

Orders, prices and availability are all asked and answered by call, so two people are on the phone instead of selling.

A dead chair closes a clinic

The dentist cancels a day of appointments while waiting for a callback, and remembers it at renewal time.

Technicians cross the city twice

Three calls in one neighbourhood are handled on three different days because nobody planned the route.

Many small dues, unchased

No single overdue amount is worth an argument, and together they are the working capital of the business.

Consumable habits unknown

A clinic that used to order every month stopped four months ago and nobody spotted it.

Warranty and contract dates lost

Chairs sold three years ago have no record of what cover they carry, so every service call starts with a search.

In practice

Four hours that matter in a dental dealership.

A chair is working again by evening

Workflow: The clinic logs the fault, the ticket routes to the technician covering that area, and the job card closes with the part fitted and the dentist's signature.

Result: A day of appointments saved, and a customer who renews.

Six calls become one run

Workflow: The dispatch board groups the day's calls by area and builds the technician's route before the first visit.

Result: More jobs closed per technician per day.

A quiet clinic is noticed

Workflow: The account shows no consumable order for four cycles, so the rep adds it to next week's visit list.

Result: A lapsed customer is asked about before it becomes a lost one.

Small dues are collected without a row

Workflow: Receivables ageing lists every overdue balance, and WhatsApp reminders go out in one batch with the invoice reference.

Result: Cash in, without a difficult phone call.

Questions

Dental equipment dealing, answered.

Can clinics order consumables themselves?

Yes. The customer self-service portal lets a practice repeat its previous order at its agreed prices and follow the despatch on a tracking page, which takes a large share of routine calls off your phone line.

How quickly can a service call reach a technician?

The call is logged at intake, routed as a ticket, and dispatched to a technician who sees it on the mobile app. Offline mode means the job card can still be completed at a clinic with poor signal and syncs later.

Does it handle hundreds of small accounts without becoming unusable?

That is the intended shape. Accounts, visits, orders and balances are listed and filtered by territory and by rep, and reminders are batched, so volume is handled in lists rather than one call at a time.

Can we run annual contracts on chairs?

Yes. Each chair is an asset with a warranty end date, and annual or comprehensive contracts are recorded against it with preventive visits and a renewal offer raised before cover lapses. See service contract management.

Do you also make software for the dental clinics themselves?

Yes, that is a different product. Practices use clinic software for appointments, records and billing; see dental clinic management software. Aura Business is for the dealer supplying and servicing them.

Can reps take orders while visiting a clinic?

Yes. The mobile app allows a GPS check-in at the practice, a visit note, a quotation or order raised on the spot, and a follow-up date, all of which appear on the account for the office to act on.
