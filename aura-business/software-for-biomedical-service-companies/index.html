In one sentence

Service as the product, not as the tail of a sale

A biomedical service company has no equipment sales to subsidise its engineers. It maintains other people's machines: a hospital's ventilators from one manufacturer, monitors from another, sterilisers from a third, usually on a contract that covers a listed fleet rather than a single serial number. Software for this business has to run contracts across brands it did not supply, dispatch engineers efficiently enough to make the contract profitable, and get spares without a principal's warehouse behind it.

The other requirement is evidence. A hospital paying for maintenance eventually asks what it bought: which preventive visits were done, how fast breakdowns were attended, what was replaced and which instruments were calibrated and when. Aura Business keeps the fleet as an asset register per hospital, the preventive schedule per asset, every job card with photos and signatures, and the response and resolution times against the agreed service levels.

The third-party squeeze

You carry the promise without the manufacturer's resources.

Every structural advantage the original supplier has, the independent service firm has to create with organisation instead.

Fleet lists out of date

The contract was signed against a schedule of equipment that has since moved wards, been replaced or been scrapped.

Preventive visits crowded out

Breakdowns are urgent and planned visits are not, so the PM programme slips until the hospital asks for the record.

Engineer time unmeasured

You know what the contracts pay. You do not know how many engineer days each one consumed.

Spares chased per job

Parts come from several principals, agents and the open market, and each hunt happens in a chat window.

Nothing to show at review

A quarterly review means someone spends two days assembling visit records from notebooks and messages.

Contract profitability unknown

Renewals are priced from the last figure, so a loss-making contract renews at a loss again.

In practice

Four things that decide whether a service contract pays.

The PM programme finishes the year

Workflow: Scheduled visits are raised per asset and shown against the contract, so the outstanding count is visible in month nine rather than month twelve.

Result: A completed programme instead of a rushed December.

An engineer's day is accounted for

Workflow: Jobs carry the engineer, the site, the time on the job card and the parts issued, so days are attributable to contracts.

Result: You can see which hospital consumes your capacity.

The hospital review takes an hour

Workflow: The contract report lists preventive visits completed, calls attended, response times against the agreement and parts replaced.

Result: A review prepared from the system, not from notebooks.

A renewal is priced honestly

Workflow: Contract profitability sets the invoiced value against the engineer days and parts consumed over the year.

Result: Either a corrected price or a contract you decline.

Questions

Third-party biomedical service, answered.

Can one contract cover equipment from several manufacturers?

Yes. A contract covers a listed fleet of assets, and each asset carries its own brand, model, serial and location. Preventive schedules and checklists differ per asset, so one agreement can hold a mixed fleet without losing that detail.

How do we measure engineer utilisation?

Every job records the engineer, the site, the time captured on the job card and the parts issued. That gives you jobs and days per engineer, and days consumed per contract, which is the number a service business runs on.

Can hospital staff log their own calls?

Yes. The customer portal lets ward or biomedical staff raise a ticket and follow its status, which removes the phone tag and gives you a clean intake time for the response measurement.

What can we produce for a hospital review?

Per contract: preventive visits due and completed, calls attended with response and resolution times, breaches against the agreed service levels, parts replaced and signed job cards with photographs.

How are calibration records kept?

Calibration is scheduled as a preventive visit with its own checklist, completed on the job card and stored against the asset, so the history per instrument can be produced when the hospital's quality team asks for it.

We have no sales team. Do we need the CRM modules?

Only the parts you want. Plans are priced by module tier with unlimited users, so a service-only firm can run the field service, contracts, ticketing, spares and finance suites and leave the sales modules switched off.
