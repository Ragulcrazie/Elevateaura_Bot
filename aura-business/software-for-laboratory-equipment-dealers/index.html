What a lab instrument dealer has to track after the order

Selling a laboratory instrument is not finished when the purchase order arrives. Somebody has to deliver it into a laboratory with the right bench, power and ventilation, install it, run it against a known sample, and train the people who will use it, often more than once as students and research staff change. Software for laboratory equipment dealers should treat installation and application training as deliverables with owners and dates, not as favours arranged over the phone after despatch.

The long tail is different from other medical trades. There is no monthly consumable that guarantees contact, so the annual maintenance contract and the spares business are the relationship. Aura Business keeps the instrument in an asset register per laboratory, raises the renewal before cover lapses, and shows the service desk the real procurement lead time on a spare that has to come from the principal, so the customer gets a date instead of a promise.

Where deals and service slip

Long buying cycles, and a long wait for one part.

Institutional buyers move on their own calendar and their own paperwork. The service side then runs on parts that are weeks away.

Quotes rebuilt every year

The same institute asks for a comparable quote each budget cycle and the old one cannot be found, so it is rebuilt from scratch.

Installation owned by nobody

The instrument is delivered, then waits in a corridor because the bench, the power point or the engineer was never scheduled.

Training never closed out

Application training is promised in the offer, delivered informally, and never signed off, so the customer claims it never happened.

Contracts lapse quietly

Warranty ends, nobody offers a contract, and the first contact after that is a breakdown call with no cover in place.

Spare lead times unknown

A part has to come from the principal and the service desk cannot say whether that is a fortnight or a quarter.

Instruments scattered and unlisted

One university has your equipment in six departments and there is no single list of what you have supplied there.

In practice

Four situations a lab dealer meets every month.

Last year's quote becomes this year's

Workflow: The institute asks again at budget time; the saved quotation version is opened, updated for current prices and reissued the same day.

Result: A fast response while the budget is still open.

An instrument is not left in a corridor

Workflow: The sales order raises a site readiness check and an installation job, and the engineer confirms bench, power and ventilation before despatch is scheduled.

Result: The instrument is commissioned the week it arrives.

Training is evidenced

Workflow: The application training job is completed on the mobile app with the names trained and the user's signature captured.

Result: A record that settles the question a year later.

A customer gets a real date for a part

Workflow: The service desk sees the spare is not in stock, sees the supplier lead time on the purchase order, and tells the lab the expected week.

Result: An honest date instead of a weekly phone call.

Questions

Laboratory equipment dealing, answered.

Can installation and training be tracked as separate deliverables?

Yes. Each is raised as its own scheduled job against the sales order, assigned to an engineer or application specialist, and closed on the mobile job card with photos and the customer's signature. The order is only complete when both are closed.

How do we keep instruments organised when one institute has many departments?

The asset register records each serial number with its department and location under the parent account, so a university with equipment in six departments has one account and six clear installation records.

Does it help with the annual contract offer after warranty?

Yes. Warranty end dates sit on each asset and the contract renewal engine raises the offer before cover lapses, so the first contact after warranty is a proposal rather than a breakdown call. See service contract management.

Can the service desk see how long a spare will take?

When a part is not in stock, the purchase order to the supplier carries an expected date, and that date is visible on the service call. The customer gets a week rather than an open-ended wait.

Do you handle institutional tenders?

Yes. The tender tracker holds the documents, EMD and deadline reminders, and records the outcome so win rates by product line are visible. See tender management software.

How is this different from your page for diagnostic equipment suppliers?

A diagnostic supplier earns mainly from reagents flowing through a placed analyser. A laboratory equipment dealer usually earns from the instrument, then from contracts and spares, so the software emphasis moves from consumable pull-through to installation, renewals and procurement lead times.
