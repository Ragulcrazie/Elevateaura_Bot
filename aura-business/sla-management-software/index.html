In one sentence

What an SLA becomes once it is in software

A service level agreement is a promise with a number in it: attend within so many hours, restore within so many more. SLA management software turns that promise into something the service desk can see. Each contract carries its own response and resolution targets, every call logged against a covered asset starts a clock, and calls approaching their limit are visible before they breach rather than after.

Aura Business holds this as SLA Definitions and Breach Tracking in the AMC and Service Contracts suite, with SLA timers also running on helpdesk tickets. It is one of the few modules whose value is entirely about timing. A breach found the next morning is a report. A call flagged at hour three is a save.

Why SLAs are breached

Most breaches are not capacity problems. They are visibility problems.

Nobody decides to miss a response time. The call simply sits in a queue where nothing indicates it is different from the others.

Different promises, one queue

A four-hour contract and a next-day contract sit in the same list looking identical.

The clock exists only in the contract

The response time is in a PDF nobody on the service desk has read.

Breach discovered by the customer

The first anyone hears of it is an angry call from the hospital's biomedical head.

No escalation path

A call heading for breach is nobody's problem until it becomes everybody's problem.

Penalties argued without data

The customer claims repeated breaches and you cannot show your side of the record.

Renewals negotiated on feeling

You cannot demonstrate that you met the terms, so the renewal price is pushed down.

In practice

Four moments where the clock earns its keep.

The urgent contract is served first

Workflow: Two calls arrive together; the one on a four-hour contract shows less time remaining and is dispatched first.

Result: The queue reflects what you actually promised.

A breach is prevented at hour three

Workflow: A call approaching its response limit is flagged, and the coordinator reassigns it to a closer engineer.

Result: The visit happens inside the window.

A penalty claim is answered

Workflow: The customer claims three missed responses; the record shows the log time, the attendance time and the signature for each.

Result: The discussion is about facts.

The renewal holds its price

Workflow: At term end the contract shows calls attended, response times met and resolutions completed.

Result: You renew on performance rather than on a discount.

Questions

SLA management, answered.

Can different customers have different SLAs?

Yes, and that is the point of holding them on the contract. A four-hour response for a teaching hospital and a next-working-day response for a small clinic both apply automatically to calls raised against their own assets.

When does the response clock start and stop?

It starts when the call is logged and stops when the engineer is recorded as attending, which in practice is the GPS check-in on the mobile app. The resolution clock runs until the job is closed.

What happens when a call is about to breach?

It is flagged on the service desk and escalated to the owner you have nominated, so a decision can be made to reassign, send a second engineer or inform the customer before the window closes.

Do helpdesk tickets have SLAs too?

Yes. SLA timers run on tickets in the Ticketing and Helpdesk suite as well as on field service calls. See helpdesk ticketing.

Does missing an SLA calculate a penalty automatically?

The breach is recorded with its times. Whether a penalty applies, and how it is computed, depends on your contract wording, so the platform gives you the evidence rather than a commercial judgement.

How does this help at renewal?

The contract carries its call history, response times and resolutions, so the renewal is discussed against the record. See contract management and AMC billing.
