Why electrical supply is a schedule, not an order

An electrical dealer is usually quoting against somebody else's project. The enquiry arrives as a schedule of items with ratings and makes, the order is placed by a contractor whose own programme decides when material is wanted, and the supply happens in releases over months. Software for an electrical equipment dealer has to keep the quoted schedule, the ordered quantities and the delivered quantities in one place, because the gap between them is where disputes and dead stock both come from.

Then there are two obligations that follow the goods. Panels and larger equipment are commissioned and tested at site, which is site work with a record attached, and a unit that fails under warranty is not your loss to absorb but a claim to make against the principal. Aura Business tracks serial numbers from goods receipt to the site, holds commissioning as a job with proof, and keeps warranty claims with the evidence attached.

Where project supply hurts

Quoted in March, delivered in pieces until October.

The money is made on a competitive schedule and lost in the months of supply that follow it.

The quoted schedule disappears

The order references the quotation, and the quotation was a spreadsheet emailed by someone who has since left.

Material blocked for a stalled site

Stock is bought in for a project that slows down, and it sits reserved for months against nothing.

Balance quantities disputed

After many part deliveries, the contractor's count and yours do not agree and the last invoice is held.

Commissioning not recorded

A panel is energised and tested, and there is no signed record of when or by whom when a question arises.

Warranty claims absorbed

A failed unit is replaced free to keep the contractor happy, and the claim to the principal is never filed.

Retention money forgotten

A portion of the order value is held against completion and nobody is chasing it a year later.

In practice

Four points where an electrical dealer protects its margin.

The quoted schedule is still on the record

Workflow: The order is raised from the accepted quotation version, so rates, makes and ratings carry through without retyping.

Result: No arguments about what was offered.

A slowing site is visible early

Workflow: The order shows how much has been released and when the last challan went out, so a project that has stalled stands out.

Result: Buying decisions are adjusted before stock is stranded.

Commissioning is evidenced

Workflow: The commissioning job closes with photographs of the installed panel and the site engineer signature on the app.

Result: The completion claim is supported the same day.

A failed unit becomes a claim

Workflow: The serial number links to the goods receipt and the principal, and the job card records the fault and the replacement issued.

Result: A claim filed with evidence rather than a cost absorbed.

Questions

Electrical dealing, answered.

Can we quote a schedule with dozens of line items?

Yes. The quotation builder works from the catalogue, so a long schedule with makes and ratings is assembled by selection rather than typing, and revisions are kept as versions against the project account.

How is part supply against one order handled?

The sales order holds the ordered quantity per line and every release is a delivery challan against it, so ordered, delivered and pending quantities are live on the order and do not need reconciling from paperwork.

Can we record commissioning and testing at site?

Yes. Commissioning is raised as a job with a date and an engineer, completed on the mobile job card with photographs and a site signature, and stored against the installed unit. Offline mode covers sites without network.

Does it help with warranty claims back to the manufacturer?

Each unit is traceable by serial from goods receipt to the site, and the failure is recorded on a job card with photographs. That is the evidence a principal asks for when you file the claim. See warranty management.

Is the platform really used outside healthcare?

Yes. Aura Business is built for any business that sells, installs and services, including industrial, electrical and building products distributors. The modules are the same; the records and workflows you switch on differ by trade.

Can the contractor see the status of a release?

Yes. An order tracking page shows each stage of a despatch, and a contractor with a portal login can see their orders, invoices and outstanding balance without ringing your office.
