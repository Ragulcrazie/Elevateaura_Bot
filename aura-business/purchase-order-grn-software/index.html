In one sentence

What a PO and a GRN each commit you to

A purchase order is a commitment to buy at an agreed rate. A goods receipt note is the record that the goods actually arrived, in what quantity and condition. The useful software behaviour is that the second one automatically does two things at once: it puts the received quantity into the right stock location, and it opens a payable against the supplier for what was received.

Aura Business handles this in the Procurement and Suppliers suite, through the Supplier Master, Supplier Price Lists, Purchase Orders, PO Approval Workflow, Goods Receipt and Supplier Payments. Because purchasing and inventory are the same system, a short delivery is visible as a short delivery rather than as a mysterious difference discovered at the next stock count.

Where buying goes wrong

Purchasing errors are quiet until the supplier's statement arrives.

Sales mistakes get found by customers. Purchase mistakes get found by accountants, months later.

Orders placed on WhatsApp

A purchase is agreed in a chat with no document, so there is nothing to check the invoice against.

Rates nobody can confirm

The supplier bills at a higher rate than agreed and there is no price list to point at.

No approval before commitment

A large order is placed by someone who did not need to ask anyone, and the money is already spent.

Short delivery not recorded

Nine of ten cartons arrive, the paperwork says ten, and the difference becomes a stock loss.

Receipt does not reach stock

Goods sit in the godown for a week before anyone enters them, so sales quote from a stale figure.

Payables reconstructed later

What is owed to each supplier is worked out from invoices in a file rather than from receipts.

In practice

Four purchasing days that stop costing money.

A reorder becomes an order

Workflow: A branch falls below its reorder level, the shortfall becomes a purchase order priced from the supplier's list, and it goes for approval.

Result: Buying is triggered by stock, not by a phone call from sales.

The rate is not argued about

Workflow: The supplier invoices above the agreed rate and the PO shows the price list figure on the line.

Result: The difference is raised before payment, not after.

A short delivery is caught at the gate

Workflow: The GRN records nine received against ten ordered, and the PO stays partly open.

Result: The balance is chased instead of becoming shrinkage.

Payables are real, not estimated

Workflow: Each receipt opens a payable, so what is owed to each supplier reflects goods actually in your building.

Result: Cashflow planning uses facts.

Questions

Purchase orders and GRN, answered.

Does the GRN update stock automatically?

Yes. The quantity recorded as received enters the delivery location named on the purchase order, with serials, batches and expiry dates captured at that point, and the movement appears in the stock ledger.

What happens if only part of the order arrives?

The GRN records what actually came. The purchase order stays partly open for the balance, so the outstanding quantity is visible and can be chased or cancelled rather than forgotten.

Can purchase orders need approval?

Yes. The PO approval workflow routes orders for sign-off before they are sent, and you decide who approves what. The approval is recorded in the audit log.

How are supplier rates controlled?

Each supplier carries a price list per item, so purchase order lines are priced from the agreed rate rather than typed. Differences between the price list and the supplier invoice are then visible.

Does this handle GST on purchases?

Supplier invoices and payables are recorded with their tax detail and flow into the accounting export for your books. Input credit treatment is a matter for your accountant to confirm.

Can we buy for a specific customer order?

Yes. A purchase order can be raised against a confirmed sales order so the incoming stock is expected for that delivery. See warehouse management.
