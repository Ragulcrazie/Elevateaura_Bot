In one sentence

Analytics that come out of the work, not out of a form

Service reporting fails in most companies for one reason: the numbers are compiled separately from the work. Someone asks the desk for a call count, the desk types a sheet, and by the time it is ready it describes a month that has already gone.

In Aura Business the reporting is a by-product. The call was logged at intake, the engineer was dispatched from the desk, the job card was closed on the phone with spares recorded, the SLA timer ran on its own, and the invoice came from the closed job. Every figure a service head wants is already in those records.

What follows is the set of questions a service operation should be able to answer at any moment, and where each answer comes from. Nothing here is a benchmark or an industry claim; the figures are yours, drawn from your own calls.

The questions that go unanswered

Six things a service head is usually asked and cannot prove.

Not knowing is manageable. Not being able to find out is what makes the month a guess.

How many calls are open right now

The desk counts by scrolling a chat history, and the count changes depending on who does it.

How old is the oldest one

A call that has been open for three weeks is invisible until the customer escalates it.

Are we fixing things on the first visit

Repeat visits to the same asset for the same fault are never counted, so the pattern is never noticed.

Is the team busy or overloaded

Jobs per engineer per day is not recorded, so headcount decisions are made on the loudest complaint.

Where did the spares go

Parts leave stock without being booked to a job, so consumption cannot be traced to work done.

Which contracts are worth renewing

Contract revenue is known, the cost of serving each contract is not, so renewals are priced on last year's figure.

In practice

Decisions the numbers actually change.

A hiring decision stops being a feeling

Workflow: Calls logged per month are read next to jobs closed per engineer and the age of the open queue.

Result: The case for another engineer is made from the backlog, not the argument.

A recurring fault is caught

Workflow: Service history shows the same asset visited repeatedly for the same complaint within a short period.

Result: The root cause is investigated instead of the symptom being fixed again.

A contract is repriced at renewal

Workflow: The call count, engineer visits and spares consumed on that contract are placed beside what it billed.

Result: The renewal quote reflects the work the customer generates.

Spares purchasing follows consumption

Workflow: Parts booked to job cards over a quarter show what actually moves, by product and by fault.

Result: Reorder levels are set from consumption rather than habit.

Questions

Service analytics, answered.

Do you report a first-time fix rate?

The platform records every visit against the asset, so repeat visits for the same fault are visible in service history and can be reviewed. Treat it as a pattern you can inspect rather than a single headline figure.

Where does engineer utilisation come from?

From jobs assigned and closed per engineer over a period, alongside route and roster load and attendance. It describes how work is distributed, not how hard anyone worked.

Can we see which contracts lose money?

Calls, visits and spares are booked against the contract, so the cost of serving it can be read next to what it bills. See contract management.

Is there a report builder, or fixed dashboards?

Both. The owner dashboard gives the standing service views, and the reports engine produces cuts by customer, engineer, asset, product line or territory. Specific report layouts are agreed during setup.

Do engineers have to fill in anything extra for this?

No. Everything comes from the job card they already complete on the phone and from the desk's dispatch record. No separate reporting form is introduced.

Can these numbers be exported?

Yes. Reports can be exported for board packs or for your accountant, and billing data can be exported to the accounting package. See Tally export.
