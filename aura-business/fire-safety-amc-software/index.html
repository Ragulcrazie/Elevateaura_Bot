Why fire safety work is a records business

A fire safety maintenance contract covers a large number of small things spread through a building: extinguishers by floor and by type, hydrant points and hose reels, detectors and alarm panels, pumps, sprinkler and suppression systems. Each has its own testing interval, its own checklist and its own replacement or refill date, and none of them are usually in the same place. Software for this trade has to hold a device list per building before it holds anything else, because the work is defined by that list.

The part that makes it different from ordinary maintenance is that the customer needs the paperwork as much as the service. Building owners, occupiers and insurers are regularly asked to show what was tested, when, by whom and with what result, and a service company that cannot produce that quickly is replaced by one that can. Aura Business registers every device as an asset, raises preventive visits per device and per cycle, captures the checklist and photographs on the technician's phone, and keeps the history attached to the asset so a site record can be produced rather than reconstructed. What the record has to contain for any particular authority is a question for your customer and their adviser, not for us.

Where it goes wrong

The work gets done and the evidence does not survive.

Fire safety service rarely fails at the technical level. It fails at the point where somebody has to prove what happened.

The device list is out of date

Units were moved between floors, some were replaced after use, and the contract still counts the original numbers.

Cycles are not the same length

Monthly checks, half-yearly tests and refill dates all run on different clocks and are tracked on the same sheet.

Test records live on paper tags

The evidence is a tag on the unit, so the office cannot answer a question without sending somebody to look.

Refills are noticed after they lapse

A refill or replacement date passes unnoticed, and the shortfall is found during an inspection rather than before one.

Multi-site contracts are managed site by site

One customer with a dozen branches is run as a dozen relationships, with a dozen renewal dates to remember.

Chargeable work is absorbed

Refills, recharges and replaced units that sit outside the contract are done anyway and never reach an invoice.

Questions

Fire safety maintenance contracts, answered.

Can different device types run on different testing intervals?

Yes. Preventive schedules are set per device type, so monthly checks, half-yearly tests and annual work each carry their own due dates under the same contract, and each shows its own due, completed and outstanding count.

What evidence does a completed test leave behind?

The job card records the date, the technician, the checklist result, any readings entered, photographs and the customer signature, and all of it stays attached to that specific unit and site permanently rather than to a visit note.

Does the software make us compliant with fire regulations?

No software can do that. What this does is keep an accurate device list and a dated, attributable record of what was tested and replaced, so the evidence exists when your customer is asked for it. What any authority requires is for your customer and their adviser to confirm.

How are refill and replacement dates handled?

They sit on the unit itself rather than in a separate diary, and fall due as work in the same way a test does. That lets a month of refills be planned as one run with the stock arranged, instead of discovered unit by unit.

Can one customer with many branches be handled as one contract?

Yes. A multi-site contract holds all of a customer's sites under one agreement, with reporting across every site, a single renewal date and invoicing raised consolidated or per site as agreed.

How do we identify the right unit on site?

Barcode or QR scanning opens the correct asset on the technician's phone, so the test is recorded against the unit in front of them rather than against a line chosen from a list afterwards. See installed base management.
