Why a building products order is never one delivery

A building products distributor does not sell to a buyer who takes the goods and goes away. The order belongs to a project, and the project decides when material is wanted. The tiles are called off when the floors are ready, the doors when the frames are in, the sanitaryware when the plumbing is done. One purchase order can therefore turn into eight despatches over five months, and the only figure that matters at any moment is ordered against delivered against still to go.

The second complication is that your goods keep moving after they leave your godown. Material is stored at the site, sometimes in a room you are responsible for, sometimes in the open. A crew of yours installs part of it. Short supply and damage are argued about weeks later. And the money follows the project rather than the invoice, with a portion held back until the work is signed off. Aura Business keeps the order, the releases, the stock at each location, the installation job and the outstanding amount on one record, so the argument has evidence behind it.

Where it goes wrong

The gap between what was ordered and what has gone.

Almost every loss in project supply hides in the difference between three quantities that nobody keeps in one place.

Nobody can state the balance quantity

The order is on paper, the despatches are in a challan book, and the balance is worked out by hand when the contractor asks.

Material at site is not stock any more

Once goods reach the site they leave the stock sheet, so shortages are discovered by the crew on the day of fitting.

Short supply is argued without evidence

A signed challan sits somewhere in a vehicle file, and the site engineer remembers the delivery differently.

Installation is invisible

The crew goes out and comes back, and what was fitted, what is pending and what was damaged is recounted verbally.

Retained money is forgotten revenue

The amount withheld until completion is not chased like an invoice because nobody is holding a list of it.

Payment tracks the project, not your terms

The contractor pays when he is paid, so ageing runs long and the oldest balances belong to finished jobs.

In practice

Four answers a project supplier needs quickly.

What is still to go on this order

Workflow: Every despatch is raised against the sales order, so ordered, delivered and balance quantities sit on one screen per line.

Result: The contractor gets the balance in a call, not a day later.

What is lying at the site

Workflow: Material moved to a project is transferred to that site location rather than written off the books at despatch.

Proof that the delivery happened

Workflow: The challan, the received signature, the job card photographs and the installation date all hang off the same order.

The money still held back

Workflow: Retained and part-paid balances stay open against the customer in ageing instead of disappearing once the job is complete.

Result: Closed projects get chased like live ones.

Questions

Project supply, answered.

Can one order be delivered in many phases?

Yes. The sales order carries the full ordered quantity and each call-off is despatched against it on a delivery challan, so delivered and balance quantities stay current without anyone maintaining a separate tally sheet.

How do we keep track of stock stored at a site?

Treat the site as a stock location. Multi-warehouse stock lets material be transferred to a project store rather than vanishing from the books at despatch, so what is lying there can be counted, issued to a crew and reconciled at the end.

Does it handle installation crews as well as delivery?

Yes. Installation is dispatched as a job with a mobile job card the crew completes on site, covering items fitted, materials consumed, photographs and a signature. It works offline, which matters on a site with poor signal.

Can we see outstanding money for one contractor across sites?

Receivables ageing runs by customer, and every order and invoice stays linked to the account it belongs to, so a contractor with several sites can be reviewed as a whole before the next release is despatched.

What about amounts held back until completion?

A withheld balance stays open against the customer in the payment tracker and in ageing rather than being closed when the goods were delivered, so it appears on the chase list alongside ordinary overdue invoices.

Do delivery challans and e-way bills come from the same record?

Yes. The challan, the tax invoice, the e-invoice IRN and QR, and the e-way bill are all raised from the same order lines, so the despatch documents agree with each other without retyping. See delivery challan software.
