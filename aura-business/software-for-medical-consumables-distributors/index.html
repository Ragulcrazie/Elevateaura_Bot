In one sentence

Where a consumables distributor actually makes or loses money

A consumables business turns its stock many times a year on a thin margin per line. Nothing dramatic decides the result. It is decided by whether expiry is managed before a lot becomes dead stock, whether the right item was picked and despatched, whether the order was captured without being typed twice, and whether the money came back inside the credit period. Software for medical consumables distributors has to be strong on exactly those four things and not much else.

Aura Business receives goods against a purchase order with batch numbers and expiry dates, holds stock by lot across warehouses, lets customers repeat their own orders on a portal, raises GST invoices with delivery challans and e-way bills at volume, and shows receivables ageing account by account with WhatsApp reminders behind it.

The daily leak

Nothing here is a crisis. All of it is the margin.

High volume hides small errors, and small errors at high volume are the difference between a good year and a flat one.

Expiry noticed too late

A lot passes its date on the shelf while newer stock is sold in front of it, and the loss is discovered at stocktake.

Physical stock and record disagree

Orders are promised on a figure in a sheet that nobody has reconciled to the shelf for weeks.

The same order typed twice

The customer sends a list, someone types it into a sheet, and someone else types it into an invoice.

Wrong size or variant despatched

Similar items in different sizes are picked by eye, and the return costs more than the line earned.

Credit period quietly stretched

A hospital pays a fortnight late every month and nothing in the process registers it.

No read on which lines pay

Volume is visible, margin by product line is not, so low-yield lines are pushed as hard as good ones.

In practice

Four small wins that add up to the year.

A near-expiry lot is sold, not scrapped

Workflow: The alert lists lots approaching their date, and the sales team is given those lines to place with high-turnover customers first.

Result: Stock converted to cash instead of written off.

The order arrives already typed

Workflow: A hospital store repeats last month's list on the portal, and the office picks and despatches without re-keying anything.

Result: Fewer errors and a shorter order-to-despatch time.

The right variant leaves the building

Workflow: Picking is scanned against the order line, so a similar item in the wrong size is rejected before it is packed.

Result: Returns fall, and so does the freight spent on them.

A slow payer is seen in week one

Workflow: Ageing shows the account drifting past its terms, and a reminder with the invoice reference goes out automatically.

Result: The credit conversation happens early and politely.

Questions

Consumables distribution, answered.

Does the system stop us selling expired stock?

Stock is held by lot with its expiry date, alerts surface lots approaching that date, and despatch picks the oldest suitable lot. What the system gives you is visibility and a default; the picking discipline is still yours.

Can hospital stores place their own indents?

Yes. A hospital or clinic uses the customer self-service portal to repeat a previous order at its agreed prices, and larger buyers or sub-distributors use the B2B order portal. Both feed straight into despatch without re-keying.

How many invoices a day can it handle?

Invoicing is designed for volume, with gapless numbering, challans and e-way bills raised from the same order and e-invoice IRN and QR where you are required to generate them. See e-invoice software.

Can we see which product lines actually earn?

Yes. Profit and loss by product line and territory separates volume from contribution, so a high-turnover line with almost no margin is visible next to a slower line that pays better.

How do returns of short-dated stock to the supplier work?

The return is recorded against the goods receipt and lot, a credit note or debit adjustment is raised, and stock leaves the warehouse with the movement on the ledger, so the supplier claim can be evidenced.

Do we need the service and AMC modules?

Usually not. A pure consumables business rarely runs engineers or contracts, so the service suite can stay switched off. Plans are priced by module tier with unlimited users, so you take the modules you use.
