In one sentence

How attendance works when nobody passes a biometric machine

A fingerprint reader at the office door is useless for a rep who starts the day at a hospital and a service engineer who goes straight to a breakdown. A field attendance app replaces that machine with the phone already in their pocket: the person marks in, the app records where they were and a selfie confirms it was them.

In Aura Business this is the HRMS suite. Attendance with geolocation and selfie, leave applications and approvals, a staff directory with roles, shift and roster planning, staff verification, and the payroll inputs that the month's records add up to.

The useful part is not the check-in itself. It is that at month end there is one register, built from timestamped records, that the person preparing salaries can work from without reconciling anyone's messages.

The register problem

Why field attendance is never quite right.

Everybody involved is acting in good faith. The record is simply not being made anywhere.

Attendance by message

A good morning in a group at 9.15 is a greeting, not a record, and it cannot be totalled at month end.

Marked from anywhere

A simple check-in button can be pressed from bed, which is why nobody trusts it.

Half-days and late starts argued later

Without a timestamp, the discussion at salary time is memory against memory.

Leave requested verbally

Permission given on a phone call is forgotten by the person who gave it.

The register is rebuilt every month

Somebody spends two days scrolling chats and calling supervisors to compile a sheet.

No link to the work done

Attendance sits in one file and visits or service calls in another, so present and productive are separate questions.

In practice

What a real register changes.

A rep starts at a hospital, not the office

Workflow: She marks attendance from the hospital gate with location and selfie, then logs the field visit when she checks in inside.

Result: The day is recorded without anyone driving to the office to be counted.

A disputed half-day is settled in seconds

Workflow: The record shows the mark time, the location and the photo taken at that moment.

Result: The conversation moves from argument to fact.

Leave stops being verbal

Workflow: An engineer applies in the app, the service head approves it, and the roster for that week reflects the gap.

Result: Dispatch stops assigning calls to someone who is not working.

Salaries are prepared from one sheet

Workflow: The month closes with present, leave and absent days totalled per person, ready as payroll inputs.

Result: Two days of compilation disappear from the accounts desk.

Questions

Field attendance, answered.

Does the app track staff all day?

Attendance captures location at the moment of marking, and field visits capture GPS check-in at customer sites. How much continuous tracking is switched on is a decision for you and your team, agreed during setup.

What is geo-fencing here?

A check-in can be tied to a defined location or territory, so a mark is accepted at the site where the person is expected. The boundaries are configured per role or branch during setup.

Is the selfie stored, and for how long?

The photo is stored against the attendance record so a disputed day can be checked later. Retention and who may view the images are settled during setup along with role-based access.

Does this run payroll?

No. It produces payroll inputs, which are the attendance, leave and absence totals your salary process needs. The salary calculation itself stays where you do it today.

What if the phone has no network?

The app works offline for field work and queues records until a connection returns, so a mark made in a basement or a plant room is not lost.

Can the same app cover sales and service staff?

Yes. Attendance, leave and roster are shared, while the screens each role sees differ. See the technician app and beat plans for reps.
