What makes an imaging sale different from any other

An imaging system is one of the largest single purchases a hospital or diagnostic centre makes. The decision involves a radiologist, a biomedical engineer, a finance head and a committee, it often runs through a tender, and it can take the better part of a year. Software for imaging equipment dealers has to keep a pipeline alive across that span, hold every quotation revision, and remember that the machine cannot be delivered until a room, a power supply and the site works are ready.

After handover the economics invert. The contract on a high-value system is a serious annual sum, the customer is losing scan revenue for every hour the machine is down, and expensive assemblies are usually carved out of the cover by name. Aura Business tracks the installed system by serial and site, runs preventive visits against it, records response and resolution on every breakdown, and reports what a contract actually cost you to honour.

Where value leaks

A year to win it, an afternoon to lose the contract.

Capital imaging punishes both a slow sales process and a vague service promise, and the same dealership usually has one of each.

The pipeline goes cold

A committee postpones twice, the rep moves on, and a serious opportunity disappears from view without ever being lost.

Site works not sequenced

The order is signed and the machine is ready, but the room, the power and the civil work were never scheduled against a date.

Installation run on messages

Several people, several visits and a manufacturer engineer, coordinated in a chat group with no record of what was completed.

Downtime disputed

The customer says the system was down for days, you say hours, and neither side has a timestamped record.

Carve-outs forgotten

A high-value assembly excluded from the contract is replaced anyway, because the exclusion lives in a signed copy nobody reads.

Contract profit unknown

The annual value is known, the visits and parts consumed under it are not, so renewals are priced on habit.

In practice

Four points where an imaging dealership wins or loses.

A postponed committee does not kill the deal

Workflow: The account carries the committee schedule and a next action date, so the reminder fires with the latest quotation version attached.

Result: You are on the agenda when it finally meets.

The room is ready before the truck leaves

Workflow: Site readiness jobs cover the civil work, power and access, and despatch is scheduled only once they are signed off.

Result: No machine waiting in a corridor and no idle installation team.

A downtime argument is settled with records

Workflow: Call intake, dispatch, arrival and job completion are timestamped on the job card, and the SLA on the contract is compared against them.

Result: A conversation about facts rather than impressions.

A renewal is priced from this machine

Workflow: Contract profitability shows what the last year of visits and parts on that serial number actually cost.

Result: A comprehensive offer that is not a guess.

Questions

Imaging equipment dealing, answered.

Can the pipeline cope with a sale that takes a year?

Yes. Opportunities carry their own stages and next action dates, visits and quotation versions stay on the hospital account, and committee or tender dates drive the reminders, so a slow deal is followed rather than aged out.

How is site preparation handled before delivery?

Site readiness is raised as scheduled jobs against the order, with an engineer, a date and photo evidence on completion. Despatch is planned against those jobs, so the machine arrives when the room can take it.

Can we prove response and resolution times on a downtime dispute?

The service call records intake, dispatch, arrival and completion with timestamps, and the SLA definition on the contract states what was promised. The comparison is on the record rather than in anyone's memory. See what an SLA means.

How are excluded high-value assemblies handled?

Inclusions and exclusions are held on the contract record, so when a call arrives the service desk can see immediately whether that assembly is covered before an engineer commits anything to the customer.

Do you also supply imaging software to these customers?

Yes, separately. Some imaging dealers resell or bundle a picture archive and viewer with the system. That product is AuraPACS, which stores, displays and shares studies; the reading is done by the centre's own radiologist.

Can engineers be allocated by skill rather than by who is free?

Yes. Skill-based technician allocation means a call on a particular modality is routed to an engineer trained on it, and the dispatch board shows the schedule and route for the day.
