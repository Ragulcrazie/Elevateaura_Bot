Why lift maintenance is a building business, not a machine business

Lift maintenance software has to model a building before it models a machine. One agreement covers four cars in a tower, or nine across a campus, each with its own model, controller, load and installation date, and each needing its own visit record. The customer you deal with is a building manager or an association secretary, not the person who bought the lift, and what they judge you on is whether the cars ran and whether you turned up when someone was inside one.

That gives the work two rhythms running at once. There is the planned rhythm, the periodic maintenance and the periodic inspection cycle that has to be evidenced with dates, readings and a signature. And there is the interrupt rhythm, the entrapment or breakdown call that has to be dispatched immediately and beat everything already on the technician's list. Aura Business holds the contract against the asset register of cars, raises the planned visits per car, runs intake and dispatch with a response clock for calls, and keeps spares against the model rather than as one undifferentiated pile.

Where it goes wrong

Six problems every lift service office recognises.

None of these are complicated. They are all consequences of a contract, a machine list and a call register living in three different places.

The car list does not match the building

Cars were modernised, controllers replaced and one was taken out of service, and the agreement still lists the original set.

Periodic visits cannot be evidenced

The visits happened, but the proof is in a logbook in the machine room that nobody in the office can see.

Entrapment calls go through a personal phone

The urgent call reaches a technician directly, so there is no logged time, no dispatch record and no closing note.

Spares do not fit the next lift

A part bought for one model sits in the van for a year because nobody knows which cars it suits.

The building manager keeps asking for history

Every association meeting needs a list of visits and breakdowns, and it is rebuilt by hand each time.

Renewals lapse quietly

A contract ends, the visits stop, and nobody notices until the building rings about a breakdown you are no longer paid for.

In practice

Four moments that decide whether the contract renews.

Someone is stuck in car three

Workflow: The call is logged at the desk against that specific car, a timer starts, and dispatch goes to the nearest technician trained on the model.

Result: A response time you can show, not one you claim.

The association asks for a year of history

Workflow: Visits, breakdowns, parts and downtime are already attached to each car under the building contract.

Result: A report printed rather than assembled.

The part in the van fits nothing

Workflow: Parts are catalogued against the models they suit, and consumption is booked against the job that used them.

Result: Van stock that matches the buildings on the route.

The contract is three weeks from expiry

Workflow: The renewal engine works from contract end dates and raises the offer while the service record is still fresh.

Result: Renewal discussed before a lapse, not after.

Questions

Lift maintenance contracts, answered.

Can one contract cover every lift in a building?

Yes. The contract covers a listed set of assets, and each car keeps its own model details, schedule, visit record and fault history. A campus or a group of buildings under one customer can sit on a single multi-site contract.

How are entrapment calls treated differently from ordinary breakdowns?

Both are logged at intake so the clock starts from a recorded time, and the SLA definition on the contract decides the response expected. Dispatch can then be made on skill and proximity rather than on who answers the phone first.

Can we evidence a periodic inspection cycle?

Preventive maintenance schedules raise a visit per car with a checklist, and the completed job card carries the date, the technician, the readings entered, photographs and the customer signature. The record sits against that car permanently.

How do we stop buying spares that do not fit?

Parts are held in the catalogue against the models they suit, and every part fitted is booked against the job card, which drops stock. Over a few months that gives you real consumption per model rather than a guess.

Can a building manager log a complaint without calling us?

Yes. The customer portal and helpdesk let a building manager raise a call, follow its status and see the visit history for their cars, which also gives you a clean logged time to measure response against.

Does it bill work that falls outside the contract?

Yes. A job can be recorded as covered or chargeable, with parts and labour raised on a service invoice where the work sits outside the agreement. What is covered is held on the contract so the desk can check at intake.
