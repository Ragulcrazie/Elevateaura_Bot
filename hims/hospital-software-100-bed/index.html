What changes between fifty beds and a hundred

A hundred-bed hospital is not a fifty-bed hospital with more beds. It has departments that run their own rosters, nursing shifts that hand over three times a day, a billing point in more than one area, enough insurance and TPA work to justify a desk for it, and an administrator whose job is the hospital rather than the patients. The informal method that held a smaller hospital together, which was one person who knew everything, does not reach across three shifts.

So the software has to carry the things people used to carry. Nursing entries are timed and attributed so a shift handing over leaves a record rather than a conversation. Each billing point works from the same patient account, so an outpatient bill, a pharmacy sale and an inpatient running bill do not have to be reconciled afterwards. Pre-authorisation and claims sit with the TPA desk. And the administrator opens a live dashboard of outpatients, admissions, bed occupancy and collections instead of waiting for department registers to be compiled.

Where the fifty-bed method breaks

Nothing failed. The hospital simply grew past the person holding it together.

Questions

Software for a 100 bed hospital, answered.

What exactly breaks at a hundred beds?

Three things. Verbal handover stops being reliable across three shifts, bills assembled from separate counters stop reconciling, and monthly compiled reports stop being fast enough for an administrator. All three are record-keeping problems rather than capacity problems.

Can different departments have their own access?

Yes. Access is role based, so a department sees its own rosters and queues while the pharmacy, laboratory and bed board stay shared. Changes are recorded in an audit trail.

Does it handle more than one billing counter?

Yes. Pharmacy, laboratory and the wards post to the same patient account, so an inpatient bill accrues through the stay rather than being assembled at discharge. See IPD management.

How long does a hospital this size take to go live?

Delivery follows a 30-working-day framework where scope allows, and at this size the scope is agreed carefully before anything is configured. Larger scope takes longer, and that is said on the call rather than discovered later.

Is it a cloud system?

HIMS is delivered as a cloud workspace used from a browser. AuraPACS imaging uses an on-premise node on your own network for the machines, with a thin cloud layer for remote viewing. Deployment specifics are settled on the call.

Does it support NABH-style documentation?

The EMR is structured and supports NABH-style documentation, with timed entries and an audit trail. Elevate Aura makes no certification claim about the software itself; accreditation is the hospital's process.
