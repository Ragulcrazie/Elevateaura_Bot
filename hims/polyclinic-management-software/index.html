What a polyclinic actually needs from software

A polyclinic has one thing a single-doctor clinic does not: several consultants working the same building on different days and different hours, sharing a reception, a pharmacy and often a small laboratory. The software problem is not clinical depth. It is keeping each doctor's patients, queue and record separate while the front desk handles all of them from one screen.

HIMS does that by keeping the patient identity shared and the workflow per doctor. A patient is registered once under a UHID, whichever consultant they came to see. Each doctor has their own appointment book and token queue. Prescriptions go to the shared pharmacy, investigations to the shared laboratory, and billing collects for whoever did the work, with the collections readable doctor by doctor at the end of the day.

Four things that stop being arguments.

A busy evening with three consultants

Workflow: Each doctor has their own token queue and the waiting area knows which number is with whom.

A patient who sees two doctors

Workflow: Both consultations sit on the same UHID, so the second doctor sees the first one's notes and prescription.

Result: The patient stops being the messenger.

The pharmacy counter

Result: The settlement conversation starts from agreed numbers.

Questions

Polyclinic software, answered.

Can each doctor have their own queue?

Yes. Tokens are issued against the consultant rather than the clinic, so three doctors sitting at the same time each have their own order of patients while the front desk works from one screen. See queue management.

Do consultants see each other's notes?

The patient record is shared, and what each role can see is controlled by role-based access configured at setup. Most polyclinics want continuity across consultants, which is the point of one UHID, but the access rules are yours to decide.

Can we work out what each doctor is owed?

Consultations and collections are readable per doctor for any period, which is the data a settlement needs. If you want a specific share or payout calculation automated, describe the arrangement on the call so it is scoped rather than assumed.

Is this different from clinic software?

It is the same platform with the multi-doctor parts switched on: a book and queue per consultant, shared pharmacy and lab, and per-doctor reporting. A single-doctor practice needs less. See clinic management software.

What if we add beds later?

The IP modules, bed management, admission and discharge, sit in the same platform and are switched on when you need them. Nothing has to be migrated because the patient record is already there.

When would a polyclinic be running on it?

HIMS is delivered to a fixed scope and goes live in 30 working days where the scope allows. Setup, data migration from your existing sheets and training at your own counter are part of that.
