In one sentence

What an orthopaedic hospital needs a system to hold together

An orthopaedic hospital earns most of its money in the theatre, and a large part of each case is the implant. The theatre list has to be planned and kept, the implants and consumables used in a case have to leave stock and land on the bill for that case, the claim has to be prepared before the patient goes home, and the record has to still be there when the patient returns for review six months later.

HIMS covers this with operation theatre scheduling, inventory with reorder levels for implants and consumables, package billing for a procedure, insurance and TPA billing, and one patient record that carries the surgery and every later review. Implants are handled as high-value stock issued against the case. How far unit or lot-level tracking is taken for your particular implant sets is agreed during scoping rather than claimed on this page.

The theatre-and-stock problem

The most valuable item in the hospital is the one nobody can account for.

When implants are tracked on paper, the shortfall shows up in the stock count, not the bill.

Implants issued on trust

A set is opened in the theatre, one item is used, and what went back is recorded on a slip that reaches the store the next day.

The bill misses what the case used

Consumables opened during the procedure are remembered at discharge, and some of them are not.

The theatre list is a whiteboard

A cancellation frees a slot nobody knows about, and the surgeon's afternoon is spent waiting.

Pre-authorisation started late

Planned surgery gives you weeks of notice, and the claim still gets assembled on discharge day.

Follow-up loses the case

A review six months on opens a new file, and the operation notes are in a bundle somewhere.

What changes around the theatre.

A joint replacement case

Workflow: The implant is issued to the case in theatre, and the bill carries it without anybody retyping a code.

Result: Stock, case cost and bill agree at the end of the day.

The claim started early

Workflow: Pre-authorisation is raised when surgery is planned, not on discharge day.

Questions

Orthopaedic hospital software, answered.

Is HIMS an orthopaedics product?

No. HIMS is a general hospital system configured for the way an orthopaedic hospital works, which is theatre-heavy planned surgery with high-value stock and long follow-up. It is not a specialty product, and anything specialty-specific is scoped on the call.

How are implants tracked?

Implants are held as stock in the inventory module and issued against the case, so the stock falls and the bill carries the item. The depth of unit or lot-level tracking for your implant sets is agreed during scoping rather than promised here.

Can a surgery be billed as a package with implants outside it?

Yes. A procedure can be billed as a package with implants and other items shown separately, so the patient and the payer see what is inside the package and what is not.

Does it handle TPA claims for planned surgery?

Yes. Pre-authorisation is recorded when the surgery is planned and claim documents come from the record at discharge. See TPA billing software.

Can we run our own physiotherapy unit on the same system?

Yes. A course of sessions is booked against the same patient record, so referral, attendance and billing stay with the surgical case. See physiotherapy clinic software.

Is imaging included?

AuraPACS is built into HIMS, so X-ray, CT and MR studies attach to the patient record and open in a browser viewer without a download. The read stays with your radiologist.
