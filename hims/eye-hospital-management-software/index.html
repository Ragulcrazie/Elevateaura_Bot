In one sentence

What an eye hospital needs from hospital software

Eye hospital management software has to survive volume. The consultation itself is short and often a repeat, but the visit around it is long, because the patient passes through registration, vision testing, drops and a wait, the consultant, and then either a day-case theatre or the optical counter. HIMS handles that as one patient record moving through a queue with several stopping points, so nobody is retyped at each station and nobody is lost between them.

The other thing that makes an eye hospital unusual is that it hands over goods as well as treatment. Frames and lenses behave like retail stock. HIMS treats the optical counter the way it treats the pharmacy counter: an item is issued against the patient, stock comes down, and the value lands on the same bill as the consultation. How far the optical side is taken is agreed during scoping.

The volume problem

Nothing in an eye hospital breaks quietly. It breaks in a corridor with eighty people in it.

The consultation is three minutes. Everything around it is where the day is lost.

Refraction findings on a slip

The optical counter is a separate business

Frames sit in a stock book, sales sit in a second bill book, and nobody can say what one patient actually spent.

Theatre lists made the night before

Day-case lists are drawn up by hand, and a cancellation leaves a slot nobody fills.

Questions

Eye hospital management software, answered.

Is HIMS an ophthalmology product?

No, and that is worth saying plainly. HIMS is a general hospital system configured for the way an eye hospital's day runs, which is high volume, short repeat consultations and a multi-station visit. Anything genuinely specialty-specific is scoped on the call rather than claimed here.

Can it handle the optical counter?

Frames, lenses and solutions are held as stock items with reorder levels, issued against the patient and billed alongside the consultation. How far the optical side is configured, including brands and models, is agreed during scoping.

How does it cope with a few hundred outpatients a day?

Registration finds a returning patient by phone number instead of creating a new one, the queue is on screen rather than on paper, and billing happens at the counter from the visit. Volume is survived by not repeating work at each station.

Does it schedule day-case surgery?

Yes. Operation theatre scheduling and package billing are part of HIMS, and a same-day admission and discharge runs on the record. See day care centre software for how that path works.

Can investigations and imaging sit on the same record?

Yes. The laboratory module runs order to verified result, and AuraPACS imaging is built into HIMS, so studies attach to the patient rather than living on a machine in a room.

How long does an eye hospital take to go live?

The delivery framework is 30 working days where the agreed scope allows it. Setup is done by the founder who built the software, in person in Chennai or remotely elsewhere, with your patient list brought in from Excel and WhatsApp support afterwards.
