In one sentence

What an owner should be able to see

An owner needs four things on one screen: what was collected, how full the beds are, how many patients came through OP, and how much money is owed to the hospital and by whom. Everything else can wait. In HIMS those four figures come from the registrations, admissions and bills the hospital made during the day, so there is nothing to compile and nobody to ask.

The dashboard is deliberately shallow at the top and deep underneath. The headline says collections today. One level down it splits by payer, so cash sits next to what the TPAs and corporate panels still owe. Occupancy breaks down by ward. OP volume breaks down by department and doctor. An owner who wants only the headline can stop at the headline, and one who wants to know why can keep going until they reach the entry.

It is also the one screen that does not depend on anybody being available. The administrator may be on leave and the accountant may be at the bank, and the owner can still see what the hospital did today without asking either of them for it.

Questions

The owner dashboard, answered.

Do I have to learn the whole system?

No. The dashboard is the only screen most owners open. Everything on it is produced by the staff doing their normal work, so there is nothing for you to enter and nothing to maintain.

Can I see it from outside the hospital?

Yes. HIMS runs in the cloud with a live demo at his.elevateaura.co.in, so the same screen opens on a phone or a laptop wherever you are, subject to the access your role has.

Does it show what TPAs still owe us?

Yes. Outstanding is held per payer, so claims sitting with each TPA and balances with each corporate panel appear next to cash collections. Claim detail is in TPA and insurance billing.

Can staff see the same figures I do?

Only what their role allows. Access is role based, so collections, payer detail and departmental performance can be restricted to the people who should have them.

How do I know the numbers are right?

They come from the registration, admission and billing entries themselves rather than a summary somebody typed. Any figure can be opened down to the bills or admissions behind it, and entries are attributed and timed.

We are a small hospital. Is this overkill?

It is usually the opposite. A small hospital has no administrator to compile numbers, so the dashboard is often the first reason an owner buys. See small hospital software.
