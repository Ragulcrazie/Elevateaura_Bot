In one sentence

What a paediatric clinic needs software to remember

A paediatric clinic has three habits that general clinic software tends to ignore. Children in the first years are seen many times in a short period, so the record fills up quickly and has to be readable at a glance. The weight taken at the desk matters at the moment of prescribing, so it needs to be on the visit rather than on a slip. And a large share of visits are not illnesses at all but due dates, which means the clinic has to know who is due and who did not come.

HIMS handles all three with parts it already has. One UHID per child carries every visit, with the weight and other observations recorded against the visit and visible to the doctor writing the e-prescription. Immunisation and review due dates are held as booked appointments with WhatsApp reminders, so the follow-up list is a list, not a memory. The contact details on the record are the parent's, which is how the clinic reaches the patient.

The frequent-visit problem

The child comes back in three weeks, and the clinic finds out when they walk in.

Paediatric practice is repeat work, and repeat work is exactly what paper handles worst.

The weight is on a slip

It is taken at the desk, written on paper and read out in the consulting room, and nothing keeps it.

The immunisation card lives at home

The only record of what has been given is in the parent's bag, so the clinic cannot tell who is overdue.

No list of who is due

Reminders depend on somebody flipping through a register in the evening, which does not happen in a busy week.

Siblings on separate files

One family, two or three children, and nothing at the desk connects them.

Last visit is hard to find

After twenty visits in two years, the file is thick and the relevant note is somewhere in the middle.

Questions

Paediatric clinic software, answered.

Does HIMS have a paediatrics module?

No. HIMS is a general hospital system configured for the way a paediatric clinic works, which is frequent visits, weight recorded at every visit and schedules of due dates. It is not sold as a specialty product, and anything specialty-specific would be scoped on the call.

Does it calculate doses by weight?

HIMS records rather than calculates. The weight is captured on the visit and shown to the doctor writing the e-prescription, and what was prescribed is kept on the record. Dose decisions stay with the clinician.

How are immunisation schedules handled?

Due dates are booked as appointments on the child's record, with WhatsApp reminders before the date and a list of missed appointments afterwards. The schedule your clinic follows is set up at configuration.

Can a parent see the record?

The patient app and portal are used by the parent, who can see upcoming appointments and reports. Prescriptions and reports can also be shared to their phone.

Can we find all the children in one family?

Yes. Registration searches by phone number before creating a patient, so siblings registered against the same parent contact come up together and duplicate records are avoided.

Is the clinic version different from the hospital one?

It is the same platform with the modules a clinic uses switched on. If you later add beds, a laboratory or imaging, they run on the same record rather than needing a migration.
