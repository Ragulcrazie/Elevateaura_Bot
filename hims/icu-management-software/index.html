What ICU management software does in HIMS

ICU management software gives the critical-care unit its own view of the hospital record: each bed with its patient, live vitals per bed, the nursing chart for the shift, the intensivist's orders and the status of the bed itself. In HIMS this is a module of the inpatient system rather than a separate application, so an ICU stay is part of the same admission that started in casualty or the ward.

The ICU differs from a general ward in the density of documentation. Vitals are recorded far more often, medication and infusion changes are frequent, and orders change by the hour. The ICU screen in HIMS is built around that density, while everything it records still lands on the one patient record that the ward, the pharmacy, billing and the discharge summary read.

Why the ICU chart matters

Critical care produces the most documentation in the hospital and keeps it in the worst place.

A bedside flow sheet is complete for the shift and invisible to everyone outside the unit.

The chart stays at the bedside

The consultant reviewing from the OPD, the ward receiving the step-down and the TPA desk all need what is on that sheet.

Orders changed verbally

An infusion rate is changed on a round; the change reaches the chart an hour later, or not at all.

Bed status known only in the unit

Casualty asks if there is an ICU bed; the answer depends on who picks up the phone.

Charges lost in the volume

Ventilator days, infusions and monitoring charges are billed at discharge from whatever the file shows.

Step-down loses the history

The patient moves to the ward with a summary and the ICU chart stays behind.

In practice

What changes in the unit.

A casualty admission at two in the morning

Workflow: The triage board shows a patient needing critical care; the bed board shows one ICU bed free and the transfer is made on the record.

Result: No phone call to confirm the bed.

A consultant reviewing from outside the unit

Workflow: The consultant opens the record from the OPD and reads the overnight chart, the vitals and the current orders.

Result: The review does not wait for a printed sheet.

An extended stay on a TPA case

Workflow: ICU days accrue on the running bill; the TPA desk raises an enhancement against the same admission with the chart as evidence.

Result: The approved amount keeps up with the stay.

Step-down to the ward

Workflow: The transfer moves the patient to a ward bed; the ICU chart and orders remain on the record for the ward team.

Result: The ward starts from the history, not from a summary.

Questions

ICU management software, answered.

What does live vitals per bed mean?

The ICU screen in HIMS shows each bed with its patient and the current vitals recorded against that bed, as shown in the hospital demo. How vitals reach the screen in your unit, from your monitors or from nursing entry, is confirmed on the call for your equipment.

Is the ICU chart different from the ward chart?

Yes. The critical-care nursing chart is built for frequent entries: vitals, infusions, medication administration and notes per shift. It sits on the same admission record as the ward chart, so the history is continuous.

Can casualty see whether an ICU bed is free?

Yes. ICU beds are part of the hospital bed board, so the triage desk sees occupied and free critical-care beds without calling the unit. See emergency department software.

How are ICU charges billed?

ICU bed days, procedures, consumables and drugs post to the running IP bill as they happen, and an interim statement can be printed for the family or the TPA at any point.

Does the ICU module support NABH documentation?

The ICU chart, orders and notes are structured to support NABH-style documentation. Accreditation is assessed against your unit's processes by the accrediting body; the software gives you the record.

Is the ICU sold separately from HIMS?

No. It is a module of the inpatient system and shares the record, the pharmacy, the laboratory and billing. See hospital IPD software for the admission side.
