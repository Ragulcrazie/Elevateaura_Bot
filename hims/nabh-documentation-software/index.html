What NABH documentation software does, and what it does not do

NABH documentation software is a hospital system that produces, as a by-product of normal work, the records an accreditation assessor expects to see: policies applied the same way by every shift, patient records that are complete and carry a time and a name, a trail of who changed what, and indicators that can be pulled for a chosen period. HIMS supports NABH-style documentation in exactly that sense.

Be clear on the limit. Accreditation is awarded to the hospital by the accrediting body after an assessment of your own processes, people and premises. No software is accredited, no software confers accreditation, and no vendor can promise you will pass. What a system can do is remove the scramble: instead of assembling a file the week before the assessment, the file already exists because the hospital has been working inside it all year.

Standards are revised from time to time. Confirm the current edition and its requirements with the accrediting body and your own quality advisers before you plan a submission.

Why the preparation hurts

The documentation is usually there. Retrieving it is the problem.

Most hospitals preparing for an assessment are not short of care. They are short of retrievable evidence of care.

The same policy applied four ways

Consent, handover and discharge are written down in a manual, then done differently on each floor because the manual is not in front of anyone at the moment of work.

Entries without a time or a name

A note exists but nobody can say who wrote it or when. An assessor reading a paper case sheet cannot tell a contemporaneous entry from one added later.

Indicators counted by hand

Someone tallies admissions, readmissions, theatre cancellations and turnaround times from registers, so the numbers take a week and cannot be repeated on demand.

In practice

Three questions an assessor asks, and where the answer comes from.

Show me consent for this procedure

Workflow: The consent sits on the admission record for that UHID with the time it was taken and the person who took it.

Result: One search rather than a hunt through a store room.

Give me the discharge summaries for last quarter

Workflow: The quality team filters discharges for the period and opens each summary from the record it was built from.

Result: A sample is selected in minutes, not assembled over days.

How do you know your own numbers

Workflow: Occupancy, admissions and turnaround figures are read from the same records the clinical team writes, not from a parallel register.

Result: The indicator and the evidence behind it are the same data.

Questions

NABH documentation software, answered.

Is HIMS a NABH accredited system?

No, and no hospital system is. Accreditation is awarded to a hospital by the accrediting body after assessing that hospital. HIMS supports NABH-style documentation, which means the records, attribution, timing and indicators an assessment looks for are produced by normal use.

Will using this software guarantee that we pass?

No. An assessment looks at your processes, staffing, premises and clinical practice as well as your records. Software removes the documentation scramble and makes evidence retrievable. The rest is the hospital's own work with its quality team and advisers.

Which standards does it follow?

The system is built around complete, timed, attributed records rather than around a specific edition of a standard. Standards are revised periodically, so confirm the current requirements with the accrediting body and your own advisers, and we will map the fields you need during scoping.

Can we see who changed an entry?

Yes. Entries carry the user and the time, and changes are recorded rather than silently overwritten. See hospital audit trail software for how attribution and edit history work across the platform.

Can we report quality indicators for a period?

Indicators such as admissions, discharges, bed occupancy, theatre use and laboratory turnaround are read from the records for a chosen date range rather than counted from registers. The exact indicator set you need is agreed during scoping.

We are still on paper. Where do we start?

Usually registration, billing and pharmacy first, then clinical documentation, because that sequence gets staff using the system daily before the records matter most. Read moving a hospital from paper to software for the honest order.
