What a hospital of around fifty beds should actually buy

A fifty-bed hospital usually has an owner who is still a practising consultant, three or four people in the office who each do several jobs, a pharmacy, a small laboratory, mostly cash patients and a growing but modest amount of TPA work. It has no IT department and nobody who can spend a fortnight on configuration. The software question at this size is not which modules exist. It is which ones go live first and who has the hours to make that happen.

The honest answer is to run registration, the outpatient department, beds, pharmacy and billing from day one, because those five touch every rupee and every patient. Laboratory, imaging, procurement and the management dashboard follow once the first set is habit. HIMS is delivered to a fixed scope on a 30-working-day framework where scope allows, set up with your own data and taught at your own counters by the founder who built it, so the owner's clinical week is not the thing that pays for the rollout.

Questions

Software for a 50 bed hospital, answered.

Do we have to switch everything on at once?

No, and you should not. Registration, outpatients, beds, pharmacy and billing go live first because they touch every patient and every payment. Laboratory, TPA and the dashboard follow once the first set is routine. It is the same records throughout, so nothing is migrated twice.

We have no IT staff. Who runs it?

Your existing desk, nursing and pharmacy staff run it, each on their own screens. HIMS is deployed in the cloud and used from a browser on the computers you already have, so there is no server for anyone to maintain.

How much of the owner's time does go-live need?

Decisions about rates, users and workflow need the owner. Configuration, data migration and training are done by the founder who built the software, in person in Chennai or remotely elsewhere in India.

What does it cost?

There is no published price. Scope for a hospital this size is agreed on a call and quoted against that scope. See HIMS pricing for how the conversation works.

How is this different from software for a 100 bed hospital?

At fifty beds one desk usually does everything and the owner is still clinical. At a hundred there are shift handovers, a billing desk per area and an administrator who needs daily numbers. See software for a 100 bed hospital.

What happens when we grow?

Modules are switched on against the same patient records rather than replaced. Adding a laboratory, imaging or a second branch is configuration, not a new implementation.
