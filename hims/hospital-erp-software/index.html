What buyers mean by hospital ERP, and what HIMS actually is

When a hospital asks for ERP software it usually means the system behind the clinical one: stock that reconciles, reorder raised on a level rather than on an empty shelf, bills that agree with what was given, collections visible while the day is running, and rosters that reflect who is in the building. HIMS covers that ground because those modules sit on the same patient record as OP, IP, pharmacy and the laboratory.

HIMS is a hospital information system with operations modules, not a general-ledger accounting package. It is not where your accountant keeps the books. It produces the billing, collection and stock figures your accountant works from, and your accounting software keeps the ledger.

What sends hospitals looking for ERP

The clinical side went digital and the operations side did not.

Almost every hospital that asks for ERP is describing the same handful of gaps.

Stock counted twice and trusted once

The pharmacy keeps one register and the ward store another, and the difference surfaces at the annual count.

Reorder driven by an empty shelf

An indent is written when someone notices a box is finished, so fast movers run out and slow movers expire.

Charges that never reach the bill

Consumables and investigations are noted on a slip and typed at discharge, so some of them quietly never arrive.

Collections known only at month end

The owner learns what OP, IP and pharmacy brought in once the accounts close, not while there is time to act.

The honest part

What a hospital ERP promises that HIMS does not do

Shortlisting goes faster when the gaps are on the table before the demo.

Accounting and the ledger

HIMS holds no general-ledger accounting: it is not a replacement for the package your accountant works in. It records what was billed, what was collected and what is outstanding, and your accounting package carries it from there. If a vendor says their hospital system replaces your accounts software, ask them to draw a trial balance out of it in front of you.

Formal procurement

Buying inside HIMS is driven by reorder levels and stock alerts rather than by a supplier master, purchase order, goods receipt and payables chain. Hospitals that need that formality run procurement in Aura Business and keep HIMS for the clinical and billing side.

What you gain instead

An enterprise ERP is designed around finance and then fitted to a hospital. HIMS starts at the patient record, so a consumable issued on the ward, an investigation ordered in OP and a claim raised at discharge are one event seen from three desks. Read HIMS vs hospital ERP before you decide.

Questions

Hospital ERP software, answered.

Is HIMS a hospital ERP?

It is a hospital information system carrying the operations modules most ERP searches are really after: inventory, pharmacy, billing, collections, rosters and a live dashboard. It is not a finance-first ERP, because it has no general ledger.

Can it replace our accounting software?

No. HIMS gives you billing, collections, outstandings and stock movement. Ledgers and statutory accounts stay in your accounting package, and your accountant works from the HIMS figures.

Does it handle purchase orders and suppliers?

HIMS drives reorder from stock levels and alerts rather than a purchase order and goods receipt chain. Hospitals needing that run it in Aura Business alongside HIMS, scoped on a call.

Does the billing cover insurance and TPA?

Yes, alongside cash, with GST on billable items. See hospital TPA and insurance billing for how a claim is assembled from the record.

Can we start with part of it?

Yes. Most hospitals begin with registration, billing and pharmacy, then add inventory, laboratory, wards and imaging. Nothing is migrated when the next module is switched on.

What is the go-live timeline?

Live in 30 working days where scope allows, with scope fixed per project. Data migration, branding and staff training sit inside that window.
