Whose assets this page is about

This is the hospital side of equipment. The register belongs to the hospital that owns the monitors, the ventilators, the analysers and the chairs, and it answers the questions a biomedical engineer is asked: what do we own, where is that unit now, when was it last serviced or calibrated, and is it still covered. The equipment sits in the same HIMS inventory that already tracks consumables, so one system covers both what you consume and what you keep.

It is worth being clear about the other side of the same story. If you are the company that sells and services equipment across many hospitals, you are tracking an installed base, not your own ward equipment, and that lives in Aura Business. Read installed base management software for the dealer's view. This page is for the hospital that owns the machine.

What biomedical departments run into

The equipment list is a spreadsheet nobody trusts.

Most hospitals know roughly what they own. Roughly is the problem.

Units that have moved

A monitor goes from one ward to another during a busy night and the register still says it is where it was bought for.

Service history in a file

The engineer's visit report is a sheet in a drawer, so nobody can say what was replaced last time.

Calibration dates remembered

Due dates are tracked by whoever has been there longest, and they surface only when an audit asks.

Warranty claimed too late

A repair is paid for on a unit that was still covered, because the purchase paperwork was not to hand.

Contract renewals that slip

An AMC lapses quietly and the next breakdown is billed as an out-of-contract call.

In practice

The four questions a biomedical engineer gets asked.

Where is that unit?

Workflow: The register is searched by identification number and returns the department it is currently assigned to.

Result: The hunt around the wards stops.

What went wrong last time?

Workflow: The service history on the unit shows the previous attendance and what was done.

Result: The engineer arrives informed.

Is this repair chargeable?

Workflow: Warranty and contract status are on the unit before the call is raised.

Result: Cover is claimed while it still applies.

An audit asks for calibration records

Workflow: Calibration entries and due dates are pulled from the register by department.

Result: The file is already assembled.

Questions

Hospital asset management, answered.

Is this the same as your installed base module?

No. This page is the hospital tracking equipment it owns. A dealer tracking units sold and serviced across many hospitals needs installed base management software in Aura Business, which is a different product for a different buyer.

Does it cover consumables as well as machines?

Yes. The HIMS inventory module handles consumables with reorder levels and low-stock alerts, and the equipment register sits alongside it, so stores and biomedical are not two separate systems.

Can we record calibration due dates?

Calibration entries and the next due date are recorded against each unit, so the department can see what is coming rather than discovering it during an audit.

Can we import the equipment list we already have?

Yes. Your existing spreadsheet is migrated in during setup, which is how most hospitals start. Anything missing from the sheet gets filled in as units are serviced.

Does it track where a unit has moved to?

Each unit is assigned to a department, ward or room, and transfers are recorded, so the register shows where the machine is now and the service history travels with it.

Does HIMS service the equipment for us?

No. HIMS records what your biomedical team and your suppliers do. The software keeps the register, the history and the cover status. The engineering work stays with your department or the vendor who supplies the unit.
