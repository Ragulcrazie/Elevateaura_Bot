What a small hospital actually needs

A hospital of ten to fifty beds usually runs on a few people doing several jobs each. The same person may handle registration and billing. The pharmacy is one counter. There is no IT department and nobody whose job is to maintain a system. What that hospital needs is the working core done properly: registration under one UHID, OP consultation and prescription, beds and admissions, pharmacy, laboratory and billing including TPA and corporate payers.

What it does not need is an enterprise implementation. Large hospital systems are sold with modules that assume departments you do not have, configuration that assumes staff you cannot spare, and a rollout measured in quarters. HIMS is delivered to a fixed scope, set up with your own data, taught at your own counter, and goes live in 30 working days where the scope allows. Modules you do not run yet are switched on later without starting again.

Why software fails at this size

It is rarely the features that defeat a small hospital. It is everything around them.

Questions

Small hospital software, answered.

Do we need an IT person to run this?

No. Setup, configuration and data migration are done for you, and training happens at your own counters. Support is on WhatsApp with the founder who built the system, which is the practical answer at this size.

Can we start with only part of it?

Yes. Most small hospitals start with registration, OP, pharmacy, beds and billing, then add laboratory, theatre, ICU or the patient portal later. The patient record is the same, so nothing is migrated a second time.

How quickly can a small hospital switch over?

The scope is fixed at the start and the target is live in 30 working days where that scope allows. A hospital that wants theatre, ICU and imaging from day one is a larger scope and is quoted as such.

What does it cost?

There is no published price, because the scope decides it. Tell us what you run today and how many wards and counters are involved, and the project is scoped on a call.

We already use a billing package. Is that enough?

It is enough for bills. It does not hold the clinical record, the beds, the pharmacy stock or the claims, which is where a small hospital loses most of its time. See hospital billing for how billing fits into the whole.

Are you only in Chennai?

In-person work is Chennai and travel within Tamil Nadu. Everywhere else is served remotely: cloud deployment, remote setup and training, and WhatsApp support. Plenty of hospitals go live that way.
