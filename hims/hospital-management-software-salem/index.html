In one sentence

One platform, two very different Salem buyers

Salem's hospital sector is not one kind of institution. It runs from nursing homes with a handful of beds, two counters and no IT person, up to mid-size hospitals with wards, a theatre, an ICU, a laboratory and a pharmacy under one roof. HIMS serves both because modules are switched on rather than bought whole, and because the training is pitched at the staff who will actually use it.

Elevate Aura is a founder-led studio in Chennai. There is no office, engineer or phone number in Salem. The workflow study and the go-live training are done on site by travel, and after that the platform is supported remotely and on WhatsApp by the person who built it.

The Salem context

A working town whose hospitals absorb the districts around it.

Salem draws from Namakkal, Dharmapuri, Krishnagiri, Erode and the hill roads above the city, and it does so across a wide spread of hospital sizes.

Much of the district's economy is transport, steel and agriculture, which shows up in the case mix as trauma, orthopaedic and occupational injury alongside ordinary general medicine. A hospital that takes those cases needs the emergency side working from day one: a triage board at casualty, an admission that starts from the same screen the patient arrived on, a theatre list that can absorb an unplanned case, and a bill that keeps up with what was used rather than being reconstructed at discharge.

The second Salem pattern is the owner-doctor nursing home. Here the same person is the clinician, the purchaser and the person who signs the bill book at night. Software for that setting fails if it needs an administrator to operate it. What works is a short set of modules, one screen per counter, and a dashboard the owner can read on a phone between cases.

Buying in phases instead of all at once

Because Salem hospitals sit at every size, the useful question is not which product but which modules first. Registration, billing and pharmacy usually go live together, because they are where money and mistakes meet. Wards, theatre, laboratory and imaging attach afterwards to the same patient record, with no migration, once the team has stopped thinking about the software and started using it.

How a Salem rollout runs

  • A visit to walk the counters you actually have, which for a nursing home may be two.
  • A first phase chosen from that walk, with the rest written down for later rather than sold upfront.
  • Existing patient, item and tariff data loaded before go-live, with your branding on the screens, the app and the invoice.
  • Training in person at each counter on your own data, recorded for staff who join later.
  • Live inside 30 working days where scope allows, with WhatsApp support from the day after.

Questions

HIMS in Salem, answered.

Do you have an office or engineer in Salem?

No. Elevate Aura is a founder-led studio based in Chennai. Setup and go-live training in Salem are done in person by travel, and everything after that is remote and on WhatsApp with the person who built the system.

We are a ten-bed nursing home. Is this oversized for us?

No, because modules are switched on selectively. A small nursing home commonly runs registration, billing, pharmacy and beds only. Theatre, ICU, laboratory and imaging attach later to the same record without migrating anything.

Our casualty is the busiest part of the hospital. Is that covered?

Yes. There is an emergency and casualty triage board, and an admission can be raised from the emergency record rather than re-entered. See emergency department software for how the board works.

Who trains our counter staff?

The founder who builds the system, in person at your counters, on your own data. The session can be recorded so staff who join later are taught the same way rather than by word of mouth.

Can we start small and add modules later?

Yes, and most hospitals do. Later modules attach to the patient record you already have, so adding wards or a laboratory is a configuration step rather than a second implementation.

How long does it take to go live?

Thirty working days to go live is the framework, where scope allows, and the scope is agreed before anything begins. A two-counter nursing home is quicker than a hospital with IP, theatre, ICU and TPA billing, and the scope document states the timeline for yours.
