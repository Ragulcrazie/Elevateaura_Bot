The Trichy context

A junction city whose hospitals serve a belt, not a town.

Tiruchirappalli sits at the centre of the state and at the centre of its rail network, and its hospitals carry the consequences of both.

A hospital here is not treating one city. It is treating Thanjavur, Pudukkottai, Karur, Perambalur, Ariyalur and the villages between them, because Trichy is where the roads and the trains already go. A large part of the OP list arrives in the morning having travelled, and intends to be on the way home by evening. The same person may be back in three months, or not for three years, and will arrive with no file and a half-remembered account of what was done last time.

That makes the patient record, rather than the billing counter, the thing the hospital depends on. One UHID per person, the previous admission visible when they return, the discharge summary and the laboratory result retrievable from a name and a phone number. Where records live on paper in a district hospital's store room, the returning patient is effectively a new patient every time.

Referrals in, and reports out

Trichy hospitals also take referrals from smaller units across the belt, and the referring doctor usually never sees the patient again in person. The report has to reach him. With imaging on AuraPACS inside the same record, the study and the signed report go out as a secure link over WhatsApp, with no login for the referrer, while the admission itself stays on the hospital's own record.

How a Trichy rollout runs

  • A visit to walk the OP block, the wards, the pharmacy, the laboratory and the billing counter as they run today.
  • First-phase modules chosen from what you actually run, with the rest left for later.
  • Your existing patient, item and tariff data migrated in, and your branding on the screens and the bill.
  • Go-live training in person at each counter, on your own data, recorded for staff who join afterwards.
  • Live in 30 working days where scope allows, with WhatsApp and remote support taking over from there.

In practice

Trichy situations the platform is built for.

A patient from Pudukkottai returning after two years

Workflow: The front desk finds the existing UHID from a name and a phone number, and the earlier admission, discharge summary and investigations open with it.

Result: A returning patient is not registered twice.

A referral from a smaller unit down the belt

Questions

HIMS in Trichy, answered.

Will someone travel to Trichy for the setup?

Yes. The workflow study and the go-live training are both done at your hospital. Elevate Aura is a founder-led studio in Chennai and Tiruchirappalli is reachable for a day or two on site by road or train. There is no local office and no local staff.

What does support look like once we are live?

Remote, on WhatsApp, usually over a screen share, from the person who built the system. That is fast, and it is remote. Nobody walks into your hospital within the hour, because there is no team in Trichy.

We take patients from several districts. Does that change anything?

It raises the value of registration discipline. One UHID per person means a patient returning after a long gap arrives with their earlier admissions, results and summaries attached, rather than being entered again as a new case.

Can referring doctors see the report?

Imaging studies and signed reports can be shared as a secure link over WhatsApp, with no login needed by the referrer or the patient. The record itself stays inside the hospital system with role-based, audited access.

Do you work with nursing homes as well as larger hospitals?

Yes. The platform scales down because modules are switched on selectively. See nursing home software and small hospital software for the lighter configurations.

How quickly can we be running?

The framework is live in 30 working days where scope allows, and the scope is fixed before work starts. Migration of your data, your branding, the training visit and the first week of hand-holding all sit inside it.
