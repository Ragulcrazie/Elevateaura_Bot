The Hyderabad context

One city, two states, and a payer mix that decides the workload.

Hyderabad hospitals draw patients from across Telangana and from the Andhra Pradesh districts, alongside a large salaried and corporate-covered population inside the city itself.

Two consequences follow, and both land on the same software. The first is travel. A patient from a district town arrives for a procedure, stays with family for the admission, and may next appear eight months later with nothing in hand. Whether that visit is useful depends entirely on whether the first one was recorded properly: a structured record under one identifier, with the history, orders, verified laboratory results, imaging and the discharge summary each in their own place and searchable by UHID rather than by a half-remembered name.

The second is who pays. A Hyderabad admission is far less likely to be a simple cash bill than in a smaller city. Corporate tie-ups, employer cover, insurers and TPAs sit behind a large share of the work, often with a cash component alongside. When those run as separate registers, the hospital ends up reconciling three versions of one admission and chasing claims from a spreadsheet that nobody owns.

Billing that carries the payer from registration

  • The payer is attached at registration, so the counter knows from the start whether this is cash, corporate credit, insurance or a TPA claim.
  • Room days, drugs dispensed, investigations and consumables accumulate on the admission as they happen, not at discharge.
  • Package billing where a procedure is quoted as one price, with the items inside it still recorded.
  • Claims raised against the same episode the clinical record sits on, so the documentation supporting the treatment is already together.
  • GST applied on billable items, with the collection visible on the live dashboard.

In practice

Situations a Hyderabad hospital will recognise.

A corporate patient admitted for a package procedure

Workflow: The payer and the package are set at admission, items inside it are still recorded, and the extras sit separately on the bill.

Result: What the employer owes and what the family owes are clear before discharge.

A claim queried three weeks later

Workflow: The billing team opens the episode and reads the timed clinical entries, verified results and dispensing records behind it.

Result: The response is assembled from the record rather than from memory.

A patient returning from a district town

Workflow: The UHID brings up the earlier admission, the prescription, the results and the imaging before the consultation starts.

Result: Investigations are not repeated because nothing could be found.

Questions

HIMS in Hyderabad, answered.

Is there an Elevate Aura office in Hyderabad?

No. The studio is in Chennai and there is no Hyderabad office, no staff in Telangana and no local support number. Setup and training are remote, a visit can be arranged when a project warrants it, and ongoing support is remote and on WhatsApp with the founder.

Does it handle TPA and insurance billing properly?

Yes. Cash, corporate credit, insurance and TPA claims run against the same admission the clinical record sits on, with GST on billable items. See insurance and TPA billing and what TPA billing means.

We have a unit in Andhra Pradesh as well. Does that work?

Yes. Locations share the patient base, tariff and reporting, with per-location and group views. Since the deployment is remote in both cases, a second state changes nothing about how it is set up or supported.

Can staff be trained in shifts without a site visit?

Yes. Training is hands-on over screen-share, planned across shifts so night counter and casualty staff are taught directly, and sessions can be recorded for people who join later rather than being passed on second hand.

Does it cover casualty and ICU, not just OP and billing?

Yes. Casualty triage, ward and bed management, theatre scheduling and ICU with live vitals per bed are part of the platform, sharing one patient record with OP, pharmacy, laboratory and billing. See ICU management software.

What does the go-live timeline look like?

Go-live is in 30 working days where scope allows, with the scope fixed at the outset. Data migration, branding, claim formats and training sit inside that window, and the first days after go-live are supported closely before settling into WhatsApp support.
