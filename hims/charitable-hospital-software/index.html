In one sentence

What a trust hospital needs that a private hospital does not

A charitable hospital does everything a private hospital does and then has to answer a question no private hospital is asked: who paid for this, and on whose authority was it reduced. Free, subsidised and full-paying patients are treated in the same wards by the same staff, some treatment is funded by a donor or a government scheme, and every concession has to be traceable to a decision somebody made.

HIMS handles this by treating the category and the payer as part of the patient account rather than as an adjustment made at the counter. A bill can be raised in full and reduced to a concession category with the reason and the approver recorded, so the trust can see both the value of treatment given and the value of treatment charged. Scheme and donor funded cases are recorded as their own payer against the admission. How far concession approval is enforced in the workflow is agreed during scoping.

The accountability problem

The hospital knows it gave a great deal away. It cannot show exactly what, to whom, or on whose word.

Charitable work that is not recorded properly is indistinguishable from a leak.

Concessions written on the bill

The reduction is scribbled and initialled, so the full value of the treatment given is never counted.

Free patients not billed at all

If no bill is raised, the hospital has no record of what the care actually cost it.

Scheme cases mixed with cash

Government scheme and donor funded patients sit in the same register as everyone else, so claims are reconstructed later.

Board reports built by hand

A trustee asks how many free patients were treated last quarter and three people spend a week answering.

Every rupee of stock matters

Pharmacy and consumables run on a tight budget, and there is no margin to absorb shrinkage.

Questions

Software for charitable hospitals, answered.

Can free, subsidised and paying patients be handled on one system?

Yes. The billing category sits on the patient account, so the same wards, pharmacy and laboratory serve everyone while the bill reflects the category. Reporting can then separate treatment given from treatment charged.

How are concessions controlled?

A concession is recorded against the bill with its reason and the person who approved it, rather than being a silent reduction. How strictly approval is enforced in the workflow, and by which roles, is agreed during scoping.

Can we track donor or scheme funded treatment?

Yes. The fund or scheme is recorded as the payer on the admission and settled against it, so cases funded that way can be listed and reported rather than reconstructed from a register.

What can a trust board actually see?

Outpatient numbers, admissions, bed occupancy and collections are on a live dashboard, and the audit trail records who changed what. That replaces most of the manual compilation a quarterly board pack usually needs.

Can a trust hospital start with part of it?

Yes. Registration, outpatients, beds, pharmacy and billing go live first, and laboratory, imaging and procurement follow on the same records. See software for a 50 bed hospital for how phasing works.

What does it cost a trust hospital?

There is no published price. Scope is agreed on a call and quoted against that scope, and a phased go-live keeps the first commitment small. See HIMS pricing.
