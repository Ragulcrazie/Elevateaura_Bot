What the MIS dashboard in HIMS shows

A hospital MIS dashboard is the management view of what the hospital did today: how many patients registered in OP, how many are admitted, how many beds are occupied ward by ward, and what has been collected. In HIMS that screen is not compiled by anybody. It reads the same registrations, admissions and bills the front desk and the billing counter have been creating since morning, so the figure on the screen and the figure in the register are the same figure.

Underneath the live view sit reports. The same numbers are broken down by department, by doctor, by payer and by period, so a conversation about why OP was thin last week can be had against data rather than impressions. Because billing, pharmacy, laboratory and the wards all write to one record, the departmental split does not require anyone to tag entries afterwards.

It also changes who can answer a question. When the figures are on a screen rather than in one person's ledger, a department head, an administrator and the owner are all arguing from the same number, and the argument moves on to what to do about it.

Why MIS usually arrives late

Most hospital numbers are assembled, not observed.

By the time the report reaches the owner, the month it describes is over.

Registers added up by hand

OP counts, admissions and collections are totalled from separate books at month end by whoever is free.

Numbers that do not agree

The billing total, the pharmacy total and the front desk count come from three sources and never tally.

Occupancy known only to the ward

The administrator asks the nursing station how many beds are free and gets an answer that is already out of date.

No departmental split

There is a hospital total but nothing that says which department produced it.

Outstanding invisible

Cash collected is known. What is still sitting with TPAs and corporate panels is not.

Questions

The hospital MIS dashboard, answered.

Is the dashboard live or does it run overnight?

It is live. The figures read the registrations, admissions, dispensing, lab orders and bills as they are recorded, so what the screen shows in the afternoon reflects the morning that just happened.

Can we get reports by department and doctor?

Yes. Because every entry already carries the department, the doctor and the patient, the reports filter the same data rather than asking anyone to tag it afterwards. Periods are selectable.

Does it show money owed by TPAs?

Yes. Outstanding is shown per payer, so cashless claims sitting with a TPA are visible next to cash collections. The claim detail lives in TPA and insurance billing.

Can we control who sees the numbers?

Access is role based. A department head, an administrator and the owner see what their role allows, which is how collections and payer detail stay with the people who should have them.

Do we need a separate reporting tool?

No. The dashboard and the reports are part of HIMS and read the same database the hospital works in, so there is nothing to export, load or reconcile between two systems.

How is this different from a hospital ERP?

An ERP is a way of describing the whole operations layer. The dashboard is the management view on top of it. If you are weighing the two labels, read HIMS vs hospital ERP.
