What procurement means in a hospital context

Hospital procurement is the buying side of stores: gloves, sutures, fluids, reagents, linen and every other consumable that a ward, a theatre or a laboratory asks for and then uses up. In HIMS this is not a separate system bolted onto inventory. The stock module already knows what each item is, how much is on hand and the level below which it should be reordered, so buying is simply what happens when that level is crossed or a department raises an indent.

Keeping it in one place is what makes the numbers usable. An item ordered, received and issued follows the same record, so the stores balance reflects goods actually booked in rather than a purchase file that somebody reconciles later. Equipment is tracked in the same module, which is why the biomedical register and the consumables shelf are not two different databases.

How hospital buying goes wrong

Stores usually find out too late and buy too much.

Purchasing is reactive in most hospitals because nothing tells anyone in advance.

Indents on scraps of paper

A ward sister writes what she needs on a sheet, it goes to stores, and nobody can say later who asked for what.

Stockouts discovered at the bed

The shortage is found when an item is needed, not when it fell below a sensible level.

Panic buying at a premium

Because the shortfall is urgent, the purchase is made from whoever can deliver today.

Goods received without checking

A delivery is accepted and stacked, and the difference between what was ordered and what came is found a month later.

Expiry noticed on the shelf

Over-ordering leaves batches that quietly reach their expiry date in the store room.

In practice

Four things stores stop doing by hand.

The weekly ward indent

Workflow: Each ward raises its indent on the system and stores works from one consolidated list.

Result: No sheets, and a record of who asked.

An item drifting low

Workflow: The reorder level flags it days before the shelf empties and it goes on the next order.

Result: Urgent buying becomes rare.

A short delivery

Workflow: Goods receipt is booked against the order and the shortfall shows as still outstanding.

Result: The difference is raised while the invoice is fresh.

A theatre case

Workflow: Consumables issued to the case drop stores stock and reach the patient's bill.

Result: Usage and billing come from one movement.

Questions

Hospital procurement, answered.

Is procurement a separate module from inventory?

No. Buying in HIMS is the front end of the inventory module, which is why an order, a receipt and an issue all move the same stock figure. See hospital inventory management for the stock side.

Does it include a supplier payables ledger?

Procurement in HIMS covers the movement of goods: indents, orders, receipt and stock. A full supplier payables and accounting ledger is not what this module is for. If you need that depth, say so on the call so it is scoped honestly.

Can wards raise their own indents?

That is how it is set up: departments raise indents on the system rather than sending sheets to stores, so the request carries the department, the item and the quantity, and stores works from a consolidated list. How far you take the buying workflow is agreed when we scope your stores.

How do reorder levels work?

Each item carries a level below which it should be reordered. When stock crosses it, the item is flagged in the low-stock view so buying is triggered by the balance rather than by someone noticing an empty shelf.

Does pharmacy stock come through the same process?

Pharmacy has its own dispensing queue and stock with batch and expiry tracking, and it sits in the same platform. See pharmacy management for how drugs are handled specifically.

We already run purchase in a spreadsheet. Can it be moved?

Yes. Your item list, suppliers and opening balances are migrated in during setup, which is the normal starting point. From then on the sheet stops being the source of truth.
