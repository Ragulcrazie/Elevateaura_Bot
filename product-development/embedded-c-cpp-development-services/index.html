What embedded C and C++ work covers here

Embedded C/C++ development is writing the code that runs directly on your microcontroller: the drivers that talk to each peripheral, the interrupt and timing work underneath them, the state handling that decides what the device does, and the power modes that decide how long it lasts.

We write it in the toolchain your team already uses, against the part your team has already chosen. That matters more than it sounds, because firmware written comfortably somewhere else and ported afterwards is where most of the surprises live. Compilation, flashing and debugging happen on your hardware from the start.

The handover is source, build instructions, test notes and documentation, ready to flash, and the code is yours. We do not design PCBs, source components, manufacture, do mechanical or enclosure work, or handle hardware regulatory certification. This is a software-only engagement, and the boundary is stated before anything is scoped.

The firmware gap

The board is back from the fab. The firmware is a bring-up script.

Bring-up code proves the hardware works. It rarely survives contact with a product.

Drivers written as demos

Each peripheral has a snippet that once worked, with no error handling and no shared timing discipline.

Everything in the main loop

Delays hold up the whole device, and behaviour changes whenever another task is added.

Interrupts nobody owns

Shared variables are read outside a critical section and the fault shows up once a week.

No tests, no notes

The only record of what the firmware does is the firmware, and the person who wrote it has moved on.

Questions

Embedded C/C++ development, answered.

Which controllers do you work with?

The one your hardware team has chosen. We write to your part and in the toolchain your team already uses rather than asking you to move, because porting later is where the expensive surprises come from.

Bare metal or an RTOS?

Whichever the device needs. Simple periodic work with a clean interrupt strategy often does not need a scheduler. Where several independent activities and timing constraints exist, a real-time operating system on your part is the sensible base, and we say which we think fits and why.

Do you design the hardware or the board?

No. This is a software-only engagement. PCB design, component sourcing, manufacturing, mechanical and enclosure work and hardware regulatory certification all stay with your team. We write and test firmware on the hardware you provide.

What do we get at the end?

Source, build and flashing instructions, test notes from your boards and interface and design documentation. The code is yours and your team or another engineer can maintain it without depending on us.

Can you write MicroPython instead?

Where the board supports it and the timing allows, yes, and it is often faster for logic-heavy work. See MicroPython development. We will tell you which we think fits your part before any work starts.

Can you cover comms and the app too?

Yes. BLE, Wi-Fi, serial, Modbus and CAN layers, a React Native companion app and a Supabase or Azure pipeline can all sit in the same engagement. See embedded software development.
