Tally vs distributor operations software: two different jobs, often done together.

Accounting software keeps your books, your ledgers and your returns. Operations software runs the work that produces those numbers. This page is about which questions belong to each, and why the answer for most distributors is not one or the other.

The short answer

One keeps the books, the other runs the day

Accounting software is the right place for your books, and nothing here argues otherwise. Ledgers, vouchers, the trial balance, GST returns and whatever your auditor needs at year end belong in an accounting package, kept by the person who understands them. Operations software is not a replacement for that and should never be sold as one.

Operations software answers a different set of questions: which enquiries are live, what was quoted and at what price, what stock exists and where, which machines are under contract and when those contracts expire, which service calls are open, which technician is at which site today, and who has not paid. Those questions are about work in progress. Accounting begins once the work has produced a document. Most distributors of any size end up running both, with the operations platform generating the invoices and handing the accounting system the figures.

What each is for

Where the boundary actually sits

What accounting software is genuinely better at

An accounting package is built around the ledger. It handles double entry, journal adjustments, bank reconciliation, depreciation entries, statutory reports and the year-end close, and your accountant already knows its screens and its shortcuts. If a distributor with a simple operation asked whether accounting software alone is enough, and the answer to every question below is no, then it is enough and buying more software would be a distraction.

What it was never designed to do

The gaps appear before a document exists. An enquiry that has not become a quotation, a quotation that has not been approved, a machine installed at a customer site and covered by a contract, a service call logged this morning, a technician standing in a hospital basement with a part in his hand. None of those are accounting events. They are operational states, and they are where a distribution and service business makes or loses its margin.

Why the workaround becomes the problem

Most businesses fill the gap with spreadsheets and chat groups: a contract tracker, a stock sheet, a pending-quotation file, a group where field staff send photographs. That works until the same figure exists in two places, or until a contract on a tab nobody opened lapses unnoticed. The cost is rarely visible as a cost. It shows up as recurring revenue that quietly stopped arriving and stock that does not match the shelf.

How to decide

Six questions, and what your answers mean

Do not decide this on principle. Ask the questions your accounting software cannot answer and see how much the missing answers are costing.

  • Which contracts expire in the next sixty days? If the answer needs a spreadsheet, recurring revenue is being tracked after the event rather than before it. See service contract management.
  • Where is serial number X today? If that needs a phone call, stock is a value in the books and a guess on the floor.
  • What did each field engineer do yesterday, and what proof exists? If the answer is photographs in a chat group, your service record cannot be searched by customer or by machine.
  • What is outstanding beyond ninety days, by customer, right now? Accounting will tell you the balance. It will not tell you who was reminded and what they promised.
  • How many quotations are pending approval, and at what value? Nothing in the books knows a quotation exists until it becomes an invoice.
  • Did we bill the spare parts consumed on service calls last month? If nobody is sure, parts are leaving stock without reaching an invoice.

If most of those answers are quick and confident, your accounting software plus a disciplined office is genuinely enough, and you should keep your money. If three or more are slow, the problem is not your accounting package. It is that operational work has nowhere to live.

Questions

Accounting and operations software, answered

Do we have to stop using our accounting software?

No, and you should not. Books, ledgers, statutory reports and the year-end close belong there. Aura Business handles the operational work and provides an accounting export plus GSTR-1 and 3B preparation so your accountant continues as before.

Is this an accounting package with extra features?

No. There is no general ledger and no attempt to replace your accountant's tools. It is an operations platform covering CRM, quotation, GST billing, inventory, AMC contracts, field service, procurement, tenders and analytics, with billing that produces valid tax documents.

Will we be entering the same data twice?

Invoices raised in the operations platform are exported rather than retyped, and the sales, purchase and payment records carry the same numbers. Your accountant works from the export instead of from a pile of paper.

When is accounting software alone genuinely enough?

When there is no field team, few or no maintenance contracts, one stock location and a small customer list that one person can hold in their head. At that size an operations platform adds process without adding clarity.

What usually pushes a distributor over the line?

Most often it is AMC and CMC renewals, because a lapsed contract makes no noise and the revenue simply stops arriving. Field service visibility and spare parts consumption are the next two. See how AMC revenue leaks.

Can we start small rather than switching everything on?

Yes. Pricing is by module tier and users are unlimited, so you can begin with the suites that answer your slowest questions and switch on the rest later. Your existing Excel data is migrated in during setup.
