How medical equipment service management works, step by step.

A service desk is a chain: a call arrives, an engineer is sent, a job card is closed, parts are consumed and an invoice follows. This guide walks each link and shows where the chain usually breaks.

The short answer

Service management in two paragraphs

Medical equipment service management is the handling of everything that happens after a machine is installed: the breakdown calls, the scheduled visits, the engineers who attend, the spare parts they fit, the response times you promised and the invoices that follow. It is a chain with six links, and a business is only as reliable as its weakest one.

Done on paper, each link is a separate memory. The call is in a diary, dispatch happens by phone, the job card is a pad in a bag, the spare leaves the shelf unrecorded and the invoice follows weeks later if somebody remembers.

The chain

Six links from the phone call to the payment

Each of these steps produces information the next one needs. Where the information does not travel, work is repeated or lost.

1. Call intake

Somebody rings, sends a message or raises a ticket. The desk needs the customer, the machine by serial number, the fault in the caller's words and whether the machine is under warranty, under contract or uncovered. That last answer decides chargeability, and it has to be on screen in seconds.

2. Dispatch

A technician is assigned. A good assignment considers who is nearest, who is already booked, who is trained on that model and whether the likely spare is in the van. Sending the wrong engineer costs two visits instead of one.

3. The job card

On site the engineer records what was found, what was done, meter readings, photographs of the fault and the customer's signature on completion. A mobile job card with offline capability matters because hospital basements and radiology rooms are exactly where mobile signal fails.

4. Spares

Any part fitted must be issued against the job, so stock falls, the part joins that machine's history and, if the visit is chargeable, reaches the invoice. Parts recorded only in a notebook are the largest source of quiet loss in a service business.

5. SLA

SLA means service level agreement, the response time you committed to. A timer starts at intake and stops at attendance, so breaches are visible while they can still be fixed.

6. Invoice and close

The closed job produces a service invoice with GST where the work is chargeable, or closes against the contract where it is not. Payment is tracked against it, and the whole event is added to the machine's history.

The record that ties it together

Service history belongs to the machine, not the customer

A hospital may own thirty of your machines. Filing service events under the hospital tells you nothing useful about any of them.

Keep history against the serial number, and several questions become answerable that were previously matters of opinion:

  • Which model fails most often, and at what age, which is the input to pricing a comprehensive contract.
  • Whether this particular unit has become a recurring cost you should be replacing rather than repairing.
  • What was fitted last time, so the engineer arrives with the right part instead of diagnosing twice.
  • Whether the machine is covered today, which decides chargeability before anyone travels.
  • What evidence you can show a customer who claims the promised visits were never made.

Questions

Common questions about service management

What is a job card in field service?

It is the record an engineer completes on site: the fault found, the work carried out, parts fitted, readings taken, photographs and the customer signature on completion. On a mobile app it replaces the paper pad and reaches the office the same day.

How do I know whether a service visit is chargeable?

By checking the cover on that serial number at intake. A machine in warranty or under a maintenance contract is not chargeable for labour, an uncovered machine is, and parts depend on whether the contract is comprehensive. The desk needs this before dispatching anyone.

What does SLA mean in a service contract?

Service level agreement: the response you committed to, usually a number of hours or working days between the call and an engineer attending. Tracking it means timing the gap on every call, not estimating it at the end of the year.

Can technicians use the app without internet?

They need to. The Aura Business field service app supports offline working with photo proof and a field signature, so job cards completed in a basement or a shielded imaging room sync when the phone regains signal.

How do spare parts get deducted from stock?

By being issued against the job card rather than taken off a shelf. The part then leaves stock, joins that machine's service history and appears on the invoice if the work is chargeable, so the three records cannot drift apart.

How many engineers do you need before this needs software?

It is less about headcount than about installed units and contracts. Once scheduled visits and breakdown calls compete for the same people, a diary stops working.
