In one sentence

What hospital mobile app development covers

Hospital mobile app development means two families of apps. The patient app lets a patient book an appointment, see their token position, receive reports and pay bills. The staff apps give doctors, nurses and the front desk the parts of the hospital system they need while moving around the building.

Elevate Aura builds both on top of HIMS, the hospital and clinic management system, so appointments, the OP queue, EMR entries, lab results, imaging from AuraPACS and billing are read from and written to one database. If your hospital already runs a different system with an API, the apps connect to that instead.

The apps ship for Android and iOS from one codebase, and the hospital's branding goes on the store listing and every screen.

Why hospital apps disappoint

A hospital app that is not connected to the hospital is a brochure.

The common failures are all the same failure: the app and the hospital system are two separate worlds.

Appointments booked twice

The app takes a booking, an email goes to the front desk, someone retypes it into the system. Slots clash and patients wait.

Tokens nobody trusts

The queue in the app is a guess. Patients still crowd the corridor because the number on the wall is the real one.

Reports delivered by hand

Lab and imaging reports are printed and collected in person because the app cannot reach the LIMS or PACS.

Doctors cannot see the chart

A consultant on rounds wants the last prescription and the latest vitals, and the app shows only a calendar.

Bills that do not match

The amount in the app differs from the bill at the counter because billing lives elsewhere.

In practice

What changes when the app is connected to HIMS.

A patient books at night

Workflow: The patient picks a consultant and a slot in the app; the slot is blocked in HIMS at that moment and a WhatsApp confirmation goes out.

Result: The front desk sees the booking in the morning without retyping anything.

Waiting from the car park

Workflow: On arrival the patient checks in; the app shows the token position and sends a notice two tokens before their turn.

Result: Fewer people standing in the corridor, and the queue on the wall and in the app agree.

Report without a second visit

Workflow: When the lab verifies a result or a radiology report is finalised, the patient receives a secure link on WhatsApp and the PDF in the app.

Result: No trip back to the hospital to collect paper.

Consultant on rounds

Workflow: The doctor opens the bed list on the phone, reads last night's vitals and writes the e-prescription against the encounter.

Result: Pharmacy sees the prescription in its dispensing queue immediately.

Questions

Hospital mobile app development, answered.

Do we need HIMS from Elevate Aura to get the app?

No. The apps are built on HIMS by default because every module is already there, but they can connect to another hospital system that exposes an API. What the app can show depends on what that API provides, which we confirm before scoping.

Can a patient manage family members?

Yes. One login can hold several patient profiles, so a parent books for a child or an elderly relative and receives their reports and reminders on the same phone.

How are lab and imaging reports delivered?

When a lab result is verified in HIMS or a radiology report is finalised in AuraPACS, the patient gets the PDF in the app and a secure WhatsApp link that opens without a login. See WhatsApp report sharing.

Is the app ABHA ready?

HIMS is built to be ABHA and ABDM ready, and the patient app inherits that structure for records. We do not describe the software as certified; the hospital completes its own ABDM registration.

What about the doctor's app?

The doctor app shows the roster, the live consult queue, the patient's EMR and lets the doctor write an e-prescription and open imaging. It is described in full on doctor app development.

How long does it take?

Live in 30 working days where scope allows, usually starting with the patient app and one department as a pilot. Staff apps follow in the same codebase, and the hospital's branding is applied before release.
