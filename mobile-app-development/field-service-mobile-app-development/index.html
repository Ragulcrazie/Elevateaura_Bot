What field service mobile app development delivers

Field service mobile app development produces the app a technician opens at the start of the day: assigned calls in order, the customer and asset for each, a job card to complete on site, GPS check-in on arrival, photos of the fault and the fix, the spares consumed, and the customer's signature. All of it is captured offline and synced when a connection returns.

Elevate Aura builds these apps in React Native or Flutter. Where the business runs Aura Business, the app is the mobile side of the Field Service suite, so dispatch, job cards, spares, service history per asset and AMC or CMC contracts are already on the server. Where you have another service desk, the app connects to its API.

The technician tracking app page describes the Aura Business version; this page covers the custom build.

The field problem

What goes wrong between the service desk and the site.

The office knows a call was assigned. Everything after that lives in the technician's head and phone gallery.

No proof of arrival

The customer says the engineer came at four; the engineer says two. Without GPS check-in there is nothing to show.

Job cards filled in later

Paper cards are completed in the evening from memory, so readings and remarks are approximate.

Spares vanish

A part is fitted from the van and never recorded, so stock is wrong and the customer is not billed.

Photos in a personal gallery

Before and after photos exist on the engineer's phone and leave with the engineer.

Apps that need signal

A cloud form that will not open in a plant room or a hospital basement is abandoned on the first day.

Signature by WhatsApp

A photo of a signed paper is the only evidence the job was accepted.

In practice

Three field situations the app is built for.

Breakdown at a hospital

Workflow: The engineer arrives, checks in, reads the asset's history in the app, replaces a board from the van, photographs the repair and gets the biomedical engineer's signature.

Result: The call closes under the CMC and the board leaves stock the same minute.

A PM round in a factory basement

Workflow: No signal underground. The checklist and readings are completed offline across six machines and sync in the car park.

Result: The service desk sees six completed PMs before the engineer reaches the next site.

A disputed visit

Workflow: The customer disputes that an engineer attended. The desk pulls the GPS check-in, photos and signature from the job.

Result: The dispute ends with evidence, not argument.

Questions

Field service mobile app development, answered.

Does the app work without internet?

Yes. The technician downloads the day's calls, completes job cards, photos, spares and signatures offline, and the app syncs when signal returns. Conflicts, such as a call reassigned while offline, are resolved on the server.

Do we need Aura Business to use the app?

No. The app can be built to connect to your existing service desk through its API. If you have no service desk, Aura Business provides dispatch, job cards, spares, contracts and billing on the office side. See service management software.

How does GPS check-in work?

On arrival the technician taps check in; the app records location and time against the call. Route and schedule views show the office where each technician is expected next. Location is captured at check-in and check-out, not tracked continuously unless you ask for it.

Can spares used reduce stock?

Yes. Spares picked or scanned on the job card are deducted from van or warehouse stock and appear on the service invoice if the visit is not covered by a contract.

Can we use our own checklists?

Yes. Checklists are defined per equipment model, including calibration readings where relevant, so every engineer completes the same steps on the same machine.

How long does the build take?

Live in 30 working days where scope allows. The Carna Medicare field-staff app, part of a 12-module platform, shipped in 30 days on React Native and Supabase.
