The local node

On-premise PACS: your images on your own machine, readable when the internet is not.

AuraPACS runs an on-premise node on the centre's own network. Modalities send to it, the browser viewer serves from it, and reports are written against it. A thin cloud layer adds remote viewing and an encrypted offsite copy, but the day's work never depends on the uplink.

In one sentence

What an on-premise PACS means in AuraPACS

An on-premise PACS keeps the archive, the DICOM receiver and the viewer on a machine inside your centre. Your CT, MR, X-ray or ultrasound sends each study to that node over the local network, the radiologist opens it in a browser from the same network, and the report is saved beside the images. None of that traffic leaves the building.

AuraPACS is built around exactly this node. The cloud layer sits on top for remote viewing and encrypted offsite backup, which is why the product is described as hybrid, but if the internet drops the node carries on receiving, storing and serving studies on its own.

Why centres ask for on-premise

Cloud-only imaging makes the uplink a single point of failure.

The reasons are practical rather than ideological. A centre that scans all day cannot have its viewer stop because a fibre line was cut on the road outside.

Every study crosses the internet

A cloud-only system uploads each CT before anyone can read it, so a slow afternoon connection becomes a slow reporting afternoon.

Outage means downtime

When the connection fails the technician can still scan, but nobody can open, compare or report until it returns.

The archive belongs to someone else

Images sit in a provider's storage under a per-study contract, and getting them back at the end is rarely simple.

Large studies feel slow

Multi-slice CT and MR series are heavy. Pulling them down from a remote server is slower than reading from a machine in the next room.

Old workstation PACS is the other extreme

Legacy on-premise systems needed installed viewer software on every reading PC, a server room and an engineer on call.

In practice

What stays working when the line goes down.

The internet drops mid-morning

Workflow: The CT keeps sending to the node. The radiologist keeps opening studies from the reading room. Reports are finalised as usual.

Result: Only WhatsApp sharing and remote viewing wait for the connection to return.

A large multi-slice CT opens fast

Workflow: The study is served from the node over the local network rather than pulled from a remote server.

Result: Scrolling and windowing feel local because they are.

An old study is needed for comparison

Workflow: Front desk searches the patient's history on the node and the prior opens beside today's scan.

Result: No CD to find, no upload to wait for.

The centre wants to own its archive

Workflow: Images stay on hardware the centre bought, in a folder structure it can see.

Result: There is no per-study rent and no lock-in at renewal time.

Questions

On-premise PACS, answered.

Does AuraPACS work with no internet at all?

Yes. The on-premise node receives studies from the modalities, stores them and serves the browser viewer over your local network. Remote viewing, WhatsApp sharing and offsite backup need a connection, and they catch up when it returns.

What hardware does the node need?

A Windows machine on the centre's network with enough disk for your study volume. The demo installer is 200 KB and runs against the demo server; after activation by Site ID the full system runs on that machine. Sizing is agreed on a call based on your modalities.

Is this different from the hybrid PACS?

It is the same product. The on-premise node is the foundation; the cloud layer adds remote viewing and encrypted backup. See hybrid PACS for how the two parts share the work.

Who owns the images?

You do. They are stored on your hardware, on your premises, and the encrypted backup copy exists so a disk failure does not cost you the archive. There is no per-study charge for keeping them.

Do we still need a server room?

No. The node is a single machine on the network. Heavy tasks such as offsite backup and remote access are handled by the cloud layer rather than by extra hardware at the centre.

Who installs it outside Chennai?

Your own IT person, with remote guidance from Elevate Aura over WhatsApp and screen share. In Chennai the installation and modality connection can be done in person.
