What a digital X-ray PACS does

A digital X-ray PACS takes the image from your CR reader or DR panel over the network, stores it against the patient, and shows it on any screen that needs it. AuraPACS does this on an on-premise node, so the orthopaedic OPD three rooms away opens the chest or knee X-ray in a browser seconds after exposure, and the patient receives it on WhatsApp instead of carrying a film.

X-ray is the simplest modality for a PACS and the one where going filmless pays back fastest. Each exam is one or a few large images rather than a stack of slices, so the archive grows slowly and the viewing is quick even on a modest laptop.

Why clinics stay on film

The DR panel is digital. The rest of the workflow is not.

Many clinics bought a digital X-ray unit and then kept printing film because there was nowhere to put the image.

Film for every exam

Each X-ray is printed, sleeved and handed over, at a cost per sheet, even for a routine follow-up.

The image lives on the acquisition PC

The only digital copy sits on the CR or DR console, which nobody backs up and which will one day be replaced.

OPD doctors walk to the X-ray room

The physician either waits for the film or goes to the console to look at the screen.

Patients lose films

The follow-up visit begins with a search for last month's envelope, then a repeat exposure.

No report on the image

Where a radiologist reports, the report is a separate paper unconnected to the digital image.

Questions

Digital X-ray PACS, answered.

Does it work with a CR reader as well as a DR panel?

Yes. Both send DICOM images over the network, and both are pointed at the AuraPACS node the same way. Which unit you have is confirmed on a call before setup.

Can we stop printing film completely?

Most clinics do. The image is on the node for every OPD doctor, the patient gets a WhatsApp link, and a film is printed only when someone specifically asks. Whether you keep a printer for those cases is your call.

Do OPD doctors need any software?

No. The viewer runs in a standard browser on any PC on the clinic network. Each doctor has a login with role-based access, and every view is audited. See DICOM viewer.

What does the patient receive on WhatsApp?

A secure link that opens the X-ray image and, where one has been written, the report PDF, with no app or login needed at their end. See WhatsApp report sharing.

How much storage do X-rays need?

Less than CT or MR, because each exam is one or a few images rather than a stack. The archive sits on your own node and is sized to your daily volume on a call.

Is AuraPACS a diagnostic device?

No. It stores, displays and shares the images. The interpretation is by your doctor or radiologist.
