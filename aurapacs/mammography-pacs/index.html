What a mammography PACS does

A mammography PACS receives the large, high-resolution images from the unit, stores them without loss of detail, presents them for reading with fine window/level control, and keeps every previous round so the radiologist can compare. AuraPACS does this on an on-premise node with a browser viewer, and attaches the signed report to the study for sharing.

Mammography puts two demands on a PACS that other modalities do not. The images are big, so storage and viewing must handle full resolution rather than a downsized copy. And comparison with priors is the norm, so the archive must find last year's study in seconds.

What goes wrong with mammography

Big images, and a reading that depends on what was seen last time.

Mammography reading is slower and more careful than most, and the workflow around it has to respect that.

Priors on a CD somewhere

Last year's screening round is on a disc the patient may or may not have kept, so comparison is often impossible.

Images downsized to share

A JPEG on WhatsApp or a print loses the detail the radiologist needed.

Reading tied to the console

The full-resolution image is only on the acquisition workstation, so reading waits for that seat.

Report separate from images

The report is typed elsewhere and the patient receives paper with no connection to the study.

Patients need privacy

Breast imaging results are sensitive and need to reach the patient directly and securely.

Questions

Mammography PACS, answered.

Are mammography images stored at full resolution?

Yes. The node receives whatever the unit sends as DICOM and stores it as received. The browser viewer opens the study at full detail rather than a preview copy.

Can the radiologist compare with last year's round?

Yes. Earlier studies are in the patient's searchable history on the node and can be opened alongside the current study in the viewer, with independent window/level.

Does the viewer support the fine window/level mammography needs?

The browser viewer provides window/level adjustment on the full-resolution image. Reading-room display hardware and its calibration remain your choice; AuraPACS does not supply monitors. See DICOM viewer.

How does the patient receive her report?

By a secure link sent on WhatsApp, which opens the report PDF and, if you choose, the images, without any login. Access is role-based and every view is audited. See WhatsApp report sharing.

How much storage does mammography need?

More per study than X-ray or ultrasound, because the images are large. The archive is on your own node and is sized to your annual volume on a call, with an encrypted offsite copy.

Is AuraPACS a diagnostic device?

No. It stores, displays and shares the images. The read and the report are by your radiologist.
