In one sentence

What a PACS does for a CT centre

A PACS for a CT scan centre takes each study off the scanner the moment it finishes, files it against the patient, lets the radiologist scroll through the slices in a browser, and attaches the signed report so it can be shared. AuraPACS does that on an on-premise node in your centre, with a cloud layer for remote reading and backup.

CT is the modality where volume and size meet. A single study is a multi-slice series that may run to hundreds of images, and a busy centre produces many a day. The two things that matter most are that nothing is typed twice between front desk and scanner, and that the archive keeps growing without anyone burning CDs.

What goes wrong at CT centres

Volume is the problem, and CDs were never the answer.

CT centres run on throughput. Every manual step between the scanner and the referrer is repeated dozens of times a day.

Patient details retyped at the console

Front desk registers the patient, then the technologist types the name again on the CT. Spelling drifts and studies get mismatched.

The scanner's disk fills up

Studies are deleted from the console to make room, and the only copy left is the CD the patient took home.

Reading tied to one workstation

The radiologist reads only on the console PC or a single installed workstation, so the report waits for that seat.

Reports typed in Word

Every report starts from a blank page, and the PDF lives in a folder unrelated to the images.

Referrers wait for a courier

The orthopaedic surgeon who ordered the scan sees it when the patient brings the film, not when the report is signed.

Priors are unfindable

A follow-up chest CT cannot be compared because last year's study is on a disc in a drawer.

Questions

PACS for CT centres, answered.

Will our CT scanner send to AuraPACS?

Any CT that sends DICOM over the network can be pointed at the AuraPACS node by AE title and port, which is the standard way scanners talk to an archive. The specific model is confirmed on a call before setup. See DICOM server software.

How much storage does a CT centre need?

It depends on studies per day and slices per study. The archive lives on your own node, so disk is sized to your volume on a call, and the encrypted offsite copy means a disk failure does not lose the history.

Does the worklist work with our scanner?

The node provides a DICOM modality worklist. Once the CT is configured to query it, scheduled patients appear on the console and the technologist selects them rather than retyping. See DICOM worklist software.

Can the radiologist read CT from home?

Yes. The browser viewer opens CT studies with slice scrolling and window/level on any laptop, and the cloud layer makes synced studies available to authorised users outside the centre.

Are the reports in our format?

Reports are written on structured templates per modality, so CT brain, CT chest and CT abdomen can each have a template. The finalised PDF carries your branding and is attached to the study.

Is AuraPACS a diagnostic device?

No. It stores, displays and shares imaging. The read and the report are done by your radiologist.
