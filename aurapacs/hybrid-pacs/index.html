Hybrid PACS: local where it must be fast, cloud where it must be reachable.

A hybrid PACS splits the work in two. An on-premise node inside the centre receives, stores and serves studies over the local network. A thin cloud layer adds remote reading, WhatsApp share links and an encrypted offsite copy. This page explains what runs where and what survives each kind of failure.

In one sentence

What hybrid means, concretely

Hybrid PACS means the archive and viewer live on a machine in your building, and a cloud service mirrors what is needed for people outside the building. In AuraPACS the node handles DICOM receive, the modality worklist, the local archive, the browser viewer and reporting. The cloud layer handles remote viewing from home or a second site, the secure links you send on WhatsApp, and the encrypted offsite backup.

The split is deliberate. Indian centres deal with power cuts, shared fibre lines and afternoons when the uplink crawls. Pure cloud fails on the day the line is down. Pure on-premise fails on the day a disk dies or the radiologist is out of town. Hybrid is the arrangement where neither failure stops the centre.

The two ways single-location PACS fails

Cloud-only and on-premise-only each have one bad day a month.

Neither model is wrong. Each just has a failure it cannot absorb, and small centres cannot afford a spare of everything.

Uplink down, cloud unreachable

Studies queue on the scanner, the viewer will not load, the reporting backlog grows until the connection returns.

Uplink slow, everything slow

A cloud viewer streaming a multi-series MR over a congested line makes the radiologist wait for every slice.

Disk fails, archive gone

An on-premise server with no offsite copy loses years of studies to one hardware fault.

Radiologist away, nothing read

On-premise-only means reading happens inside the building or through a VPN someone has to maintain.

Referrers cannot see anything

Without a cloud endpoint, sharing a study means a CD or a printed film again.

Power cut in the middle of a scan

Any PACS goes dark without power, but what matters is how quickly the node is back and whether anything was lost.

Failure modes

What still works, failure by failure.

🔌Internet down

  • Scanning and DICOM receive continue
  • Local viewing and reporting continue
  • Worklist continues
  • Sharing and remote reading wait, then catch up

💧Internet slow

  • Local reads are unaffected
  • Sync runs in the background
  • Remote readers see the study once it has synced

💾Node hardware fails

  • Encrypted offsite copy still exists
  • Remote viewing of synced studies continues
  • Node rebuilt and restored from backup

⚡Power cut

  • Node resumes on restart
  • Modalities resend or re-queue studies
  • Nothing typed twice thanks to the worklist

In practice

A week at a hybrid centre.

Monday, fibre cut on the main road

Workflow: The morning's CT and ultrasound run against the node. Reports are finalised locally.

Result: When the line returns in the evening, the backlog of share links goes out at once.

Wednesday, radiologist at a conference

Workflow: She opens the day's MR studies in a browser from her hotel through the cloud layer.

Result: Reports are signed the same day.

Friday, referrer asks for images

Workflow: Front desk sends the study's secure link on WhatsApp.

Result: The physician opens the images and PDF on a phone without any login.

Any day, the question of ownership

Workflow: The archive is on the centre's own machine, with an encrypted copy offsite.

Result: No per-study rent, and a copy that survives a disk failure.

Questions

Hybrid PACS, answered.

Is hybrid two products or one?

One. AuraPACS installs as a node on your network and the cloud layer is part of the same system. You do not buy an on-premise licence and then a cloud subscription from someone else.

What exactly stops when the internet drops?

Remote reading from outside the centre, WhatsApp share links and the offsite sync. Receiving studies, the worklist, local viewing and reporting all continue on the node. See on-premise PACS for the node in detail.

How does the offsite copy work?

Each study on the node is copied, encrypted, to the cloud layer when the connection is available. If the node's disk fails, the archive is restored from that copy.

Can a radiologist read from home?

Yes. Authorised users open synced studies in a browser from anywhere, with role-based access and every view audited. See remote reading.

Do we need to manage the cloud ourselves?

No. The cloud layer is operated as part of AuraPACS. Your IT person installs the node with remote guidance and points the modalities at it; the rest is configured for you.

Is the hybrid setup slower than pure on-premise?

Local reading is the same, because the viewer serves from the node. The sync to the cloud runs in the background and does not sit between the scanner and the reading screen.
