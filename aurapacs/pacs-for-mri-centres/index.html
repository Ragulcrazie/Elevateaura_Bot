What a PACS changes for an MRI centre

A PACS for an MRI centre receives every series of every exam from the scanner, stores it against the patient, and lets the radiologist read it wherever they are, with the earlier exam beside it. AuraPACS does that from a node on your network with a cloud layer for reading outside the centre.

The thing that makes MRI different from other modalities is the length of the study and the reading arrangement. Exams have many series, each with its own contrast and orientation, and most standalone MRI centres in India rely on a radiologist who visits on set days or reads remotely. Report turnaround is the number the centre's referrers judge it on.

What goes wrong at MRI centres

Long studies, one reader, and a reporting queue that grows overnight.

MRI centres rarely have a full-time radiologist on site. Everything about the workflow has to assume the reader is somewhere else.

Reports wait for the visit

Studies pile up until the radiologist comes on Tuesday and Friday, so a Wednesday patient waits days.

Series scattered on the console

A lumbar spine exam has several sequences; if one is missed on the CD the report is incomplete.

No prior for comparison

A follow-up brain MR six months later cannot be compared because the earlier exam is on a disc the patient kept.

Remote reading by VPN

The radiologist needs installed software and a VPN that somebody has to keep working.

Report typed elsewhere, attached by hand

The report is written in a document, emailed to the centre and printed. It is not on the study.

The referrer only sees paper

The neurologist who ordered the scan reads a printed summary without the images.

Questions

PACS for MRI centres, answered.

Can our visiting radiologist read from home?

Yes. Studies synced through the cloud layer open in a browser with slice scrolling and window/level, with nothing to install. Access is role-based and every view is audited. See remote reading software.

Are all sequences of an exam kept together?

Yes. The node receives every series the scanner sends and files them under one study for the patient, so the reader moves between sequences without hunting for a missing one.

How do we compare with the previous MRI?

The patient's full history is searchable. An earlier exam stored on the node can be opened alongside the current one in the browser viewer.

How fast can reports go out?

As soon as the radiologist signs. The finalised PDF is attached to the study and the secure link can be sent on WhatsApp straight away, so turnaround is set by the reader's availability rather than by couriers.

Does this replace a teleradiology service?

It gives your own radiologist remote reading. If you also use a third-party reporting service, that is a separate arrangement; AuraPACS is not a reporting service. See cloud vs on-premise PACS for the deployment trade-offs.

Is AuraPACS a diagnostic device?

No. It stores, displays and shares imaging. The clinical read and the report are the radiologist's.
