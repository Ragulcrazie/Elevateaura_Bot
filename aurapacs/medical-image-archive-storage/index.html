What the archive in AuraPACS is

The archive is the part of a PACS that keeps the DICOM files and the index that makes them findable. AuraPACS writes every received study to the on-premise node and records it by patient, study and series, so the front desk can search a name, a date or a modality and open the result. An encrypted copy of each study is sent offsite through the cloud layer.

Storage is not the same as a folder of files. A folder cannot tell you which of a patient's four studies is the follow-up, cannot serve a series to a browser viewer, and cannot show a radiologist the prior beside today's scan. The index does that, and the archive is the index plus the files it points to.

How images get lost

Most centres do not lose images dramatically. They lose them one console at a time.

A scanner is not an archive, and a folder on a PC is not a backup.

The console disk fills

Old studies are deleted from the CT to keep scanning. The only copy left is whatever the patient took home.

Folders named by date

Exports sit in folders by month with no way to search by patient, so a prior is found by memory or not at all.

One disk, no copy

An on-premise server with no offsite backup loses the whole history to one hardware fault.

Cloud storage with per-study rent

A provider holds the archive and bills for it monthly. Stop paying and access ends.

Nobody planned for growth

CT and mammography studies are large. A disk sized for the first year is full in the second.

Retention is nobody's decision

How long studies must be kept is not written down, so nothing is ever deleted and nothing is ever verified.

Storage planning

How to think about disk before you buy it.

Count studies per modality per day

Workflow: CT and MR studies are multi-slice and large; mammography images are large singles; X-ray is a few images; ultrasound is many small frames.

Result: A rough annual figure per modality, which is what the node's disk is sized against.

Decide what stays hot

Workflow: Recent studies must be instant on the local network; older ones can be slower but must still be findable.

Result: A disk plan that keeps the working set fast without buying for ten years on day one.

Write down the retention rule

Workflow: Decide how many years studies are kept and who may delete, then apply it to the archive.

Result: A policy your advisers can review, rather than an accident.

Test the restore

Workflow: Confirm that a study can be restored from the encrypted offsite copy before you need to.

Result: A backup that has been proven, not assumed.

Questions

Medical image storage, answered.

Where are the images physically stored?

On the on-premise node, a machine on your own network. An encrypted backup copy is kept offsite through the AuraPACS cloud layer so a disk failure at the centre does not lose the archive.

How much disk do we need?

It depends on modalities and daily volume. CT, MR and mammography studies are large; X-ray and ultrasound are smaller. The node is sized against your numbers on a call, and disk can be added as the archive grows.

Can we find a study from years ago?

Yes. Every study is indexed by patient, study and series, so a search by name, ID or date returns it with its report and it opens in the browser viewer. See PACS software.

Who decides how long studies are kept?

You do. Retention is on your own hardware and under your policy. Health-record retention rules vary, so confirm the period that applies to you with your advisers and set the archive accordingly.

Is the archive the same as cloud storage?

No. Cloud storage holds files; an archive indexes them, serves them to a viewer, keeps the report with the study and shows priors. See PACS vs cloud storage.

What happens if the node fails?

The encrypted offsite copy is used to restore the archive onto replacement hardware, and remote viewing of already-synced studies continues through the cloud layer while that happens.
