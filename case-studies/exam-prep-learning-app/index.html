The exam-prep app: an Android product, Telegram bots and content automation.

Before Aura Learn was a product for other institutes, it was Elevate Aura's own government-exam-prep app: live on Google Play, with Telegram practice bots and automated content, built end to end on Flutter, Python and Supabase.

In one sentence

What was built, and for whom

This one was built for Elevate Aura itself. A government-exam-preparation product, self-funded and shipped end to end: an Android app live on Google Play, Telegram practice bots, and content automation behind both, on Flutter, Python and Supabase.

It matters to anyone evaluating Aura Learn for one reason. The learning platform now offered to coaching institutes is not a first attempt at the category. It is the productised version of something the founder already built, published and ran.

Before

The situation

The brief was set by the founder rather than a client: build and run a learning product end to end, as a self-funded EdTech build. That means every part of the problem lands on the same person, including the parts a services project usually leaves to the customer.

Two of those parts shape everything else. The first is distribution: an app has to be published and updated on Google Play, which is a process rather than a build step. The second is content: exam preparation runs on a constant supply of practice questions, and preparing them by hand does not scale past the first few weeks.

The build

What was built

Three pieces, each doing a different job.

  • Android app. Built in Flutter and published on Google Play, where it is live. The app is where a learner works through material and practice.
  • Telegram practice bots. Practice delivered where learners already spend their time, so daily practice does not depend on remembering to open an app.
  • Content automation. Python pipelines that prepare and keep the practice material flowing, rather than an editor assembling each set by hand.
  • Supabase. The shared data layer behind the app and the bots, so both work from the same content and the same learner records.

The combination is the point. An app alone gets installed and forgotten; a bot alone has nowhere to hold structured material; automation is what makes both worth keeping open.

The method

How the work ran

The same 30-working-day framework Elevate Aura uses on client projects, applied to its own product.

Discovery

Scope fixed first: what a learner does daily, what the app owns, what the bot owns, and what has to be automated rather than written each week.

Configure

The app, the bots and the content pipeline are built and wired to one data layer, so a change to the material appears in both places.

Migrate

Existing question material is brought into a structured question bank rather than left as documents, which is what makes automated practice possible at all.

Train

On a client build this is where staff learn the dashboard. Here it was the publishing and release process on Google Play, which is the part most first-time app owners underestimate.

After

What it changed

For the product, the change is that practice happens without being organised each time. Material is structured once, delivered through both the app and the bots, and refreshed by automation rather than by an editor, so the daily loop keeps running on its own.

For Elevate Aura, the change is what it made possible next. The parts proven here, a learner mobile app, auto-graded practice from a question bank, Telegram nudges and content automation, are the same parts institutes ask for, and they became Aura Learn: a learner app and an instructor dashboard with courses, quizzes and mock tests, batches, certificates, and GST payments.

It is also the reason the answer to whether the builder has shipped a learning product is a link rather than a claim.

Questions

About this project

Whose product is this?

Elevate Aura's own. It was self-funded and built end to end by the founder, Ragul Sekar, rather than delivered for a client. The Android app is live on Google Play.

What was the stack?

Flutter for the Android app, Python for the content automation and the Telegram practice bots, and Supabase as the shared data layer behind both channels.

Why Telegram bots as well as an app?

Because daily practice works best where the learner already is. The bot delivers practice without requiring anyone to open an app first, while the app holds the structured material and progress.

How does this relate to Aura Learn?

Aura Learn is the productised version for coaching institutes, exam-prep businesses and companies training staff: a learner app plus an instructor dashboard, with courses, quizzes and mock tests, batches, certificates and GST payments.

Can you build a learning app for our institute?

Yes, either as Aura Learn configured for your batches and content, or as a custom build. Both are scoped on a call. See custom LMS development or the demo.
